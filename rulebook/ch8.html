      Introduction

      From the preface to "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      This book is a field survey of the peoples of Terrana, compiled over many years of travel for Monsera University. Each entry records what I observed, who I spoke with, and where my earlier assumptions proved wrong. Where I have not met a people myself, I say so. Where a people asked me to keep something private, I have.

      Every person in Terrana inherits things they did not choose: a lifespan, a body, a reputation, and often a small piece of magic carried in the blood. That inheritance is the subject of this book.

      How Heritages Work

      Your heritage partially shapes what you are naturally good at, what you carry with you that you did not choose, and how the world tends to see you before you have had a chance to make any impression of your own.

      Every heritage in this chapter gives your character the following. Choose your heritage at Step 4 of character creation and record each of them on your sheet.

      Innate Attribute: One attribute starts at 2 dots and has its maximum raised by 1 beyond the column cap. Note Innate next to it on your sheet. Most heritages offer two attributes to choose between. Hyumans and born Vampires may choose any attribute.

      Innate Flaw: The Innate Flaw is not chosen and grants no extra dots. It is simply part of who your character is.

      Attribute Flaw: Every heritage except Hyumans and Vampires is naturally weaker in one attribute group: Physical, Social, or Mental. That group can never be your Affinity column; it can only be your Standard or Aversion column. This is separate from your Innate Flaw, and Merits can still raise attributes in the flawed group.

      Heritage Merit: Choose one of the merits listed for your heritage and record it in your Merits, Flaws, and Backgrounds section. You only receive a free dot if the merit itself says so.

      Heritage Magic: Every character begins with 1 free dot in the Heritage magic of their heritage. The rules for Heritage magic follow below.

      Lifespan: Every heritage has one of four lifespans: 100, 200, 400, or 1000 years. A few are Immortal, meaning no one truly knows how long they live, such as slimes, vampires, and the Immortal Demon Kings.

      Common and Rare Heritages

      Heritages are divided into Common and Rare based on how many of them live across Terrana and how often the rest of the world encounters them. Common Heritages are available to every player. Choosing a Rare Heritage makes your character an unusual sight almost everywhere they go, and it requires Storyteller approval before character creation begins.

      Heritage Magic

      Long before anyone in Terrana studies a school of magic, most of them already know a little of it. A Beastfolk child howls and the birds scatter. A Dwarven apprentice lays a hand on a cracked ingot and feels where the flaw runs. Nobody taught them. The magic was in the blood, waiting.

      Heritage magic is that inheritance. It is a discipline of its own, separate from the seven casting schools of the heptagram and separate from Battle Aura. It has no school color and no place on the Mages Guild star. It is not learned in a classroom. It wakes up. Every technique in a Heritage magic is a spell, follows the same rules as any other spell, and costs mana.

      Your Starting Dot

      Every character begins with 1 free dot in the Heritage magic their heritage provides. Record it in the Magical Knowledges section of your sheet.

      Any character may instead give up that free dot and take 1 dot in any casting school (Fire, Water, Air, Earth, Nature, Bright, or Shade) or any circle magic. This is a permanent choice. The character can still raise their Heritage magic later with XP, starting from zero.

      Either way, a character receives only one free dot. At creation, Heritage magic can be raised like a Magical Knowledge, with the Magical Knowledge dots from Step 11 or with Freebie points (4 per dot), up to 5 dots.

      Raising Heritage Magic

      Heritage magic is rated from 1 to 7 and uses the same rank names as every other magic.

      • 1 Core
      • 2 Terrestrial
      • 3 Aerial
      • 4 Lunar
      • 5 Stellar
      • 6 Galactic
      • 7 Cosmic

      After creation, raising Heritage magic costs XP equal to the new dot x5, the same as a Magical Knowledge.

      Choosing Spells

      Every rank offers two spells, Option A and Option B. Each time you gain a dot, choose one of that rank's two spells. The choice is permanent. The spell you did not choose can never be learned, with XP or any other way. Hyumans are the one exception (see Adaptation). Two characters of the same heritage can carry the same bloodline and still express it very differently.

      Reading a Spell

      Every spell is written out in the same format.

      Mana Cost: What the spell costs. Every Heritage magic spell costs mana equal to its rank: 1 at Core, 2 at Terrestrial, and so on up to 7 at Cosmic.

      Speed: Instant spells take effect on your action. Reaction spells are cast in response to something that happens outside your turn. Extended spells keep working after they are cast. Downtime spells can only be cast outside combat.

      Type: What the spell does: Attack, Defense, Debuff, Enhancement, Healing, Movement, Utility, or Conjuration. Persistent, Attuned, or Countdown is added when the spell lasts.

      Range: Who or what the spell can affect.

      Dice Pool: What you roll. "None" means the spell takes effect without a roll.

      Defense: What the target rolls against the spell. "Target's choice of defense" means the target defends against the attack however they normally would. A named pool, such as Composure + Insight, means the target rolls that pool against your successes and the spell only takes hold if you rolled more. "None" means nothing rolls against it.

      Use: Whether the spell works in combat, outside combat, or both.

      Effect: Exactly what happens.

      Rolling and Mana

      A Heritage magic dice pool is Attribute + Ability + Heritage dots. Each Heritage magic lists a Primary pool and a Secondary pool, and each spell says which one it uses. Heritage dots always means your dots in the Heritage magic the spell belongs to.

      You may spend extra mana beyond a spell's cost to add 1 die per mana, declared before rolling, up to your Heritage dots or Max Mana Per Turn, whichever comes first. This limit applies any time you spend mana to boost a spell, including spells whose damage value is set by successes plus mana spent. Each Heritage magic has one fixed mana assignment that says whether that extra mana can go to the attack roll, the damage roll, or either.

      Unless a spell says otherwise, an Attack spell resolves like any magic attack. The target chooses their own defense, and a hit carries into the damage roll as normal. When a spell says a target resists, they roll the listed pool against your successes, and the spell only takes hold if you rolled more.

      When a spell lets you resist something with magic, such as being knocked down or moved, you spend the spell's mana and roll the dice pool it names. Your Heritage dots are already part of that pool.

      Persistent, Attuned, and Countdown Spells

      Some spells last beyond the moment they are cast. They work in one of three ways.

      Persistent: Pay the mana cost to cast it. To keep it running, pay upkeep during each Upkeep Phase. Upkeep is always the spell's mana cost minus 1, so a Core spell costs nothing to maintain and a Cosmic spell costs 6. Stop paying and it ends. Outside combat, a Persistent spell lasts one scene for its casting cost.

      Attuned: Attuned spells work like attuning to an item. Pay the mana cost from your Max Mana Per Turn and leave it there, committed to the spell. The spell stays active as long as that mana stays committed, and that mana cannot be used for anything else in the meantime. You may end an Attuned spell at any time, and the committed mana returns to your Max Mana Per Turn. Use the Committed track on your combat mat to keep count.

      Countdown: Countdown spells hold a target for a limited time. Pay the mana cost to cast it, adding extra mana if you wish. The spell lasts a number of turns equal to half the total mana spent on it, rounded up. One turn comes off the countdown during each Upkeep Phase. During any Upkeep Phase you may pay the spell's cost minus 1 to freeze the countdown, so that turn does not come off. The spell ends when the countdown runs out or when the ending condition in its Effect happens, whichever comes first. Outside combat, a Countdown spell lasts one scene for its casting cost, like a Persistent spell, unless its ending condition happens first.

      Conjurations

      A Conjuration spell creates something that acts on its own: an awakened tree, an imagined creature, a risen body. A conjuration is always Persistent. It has no Health track and no character sheet of its own. During each Upkeep Phase, after its upkeep is paid, it may take the action its spell describes, rolling your dice pool for that spell. A conjuration ends when its upkeep is not paid, when you are Incapacitated, or when another spell banishes it.

      What Heritage Magic Is Not

      Heritage magic has no element. It never gains elemental advantage, never suffers elemental disadvantage, and never triggers elemental interactions with shields, walls, or summons. It cannot be used as part of combination magic.

      A character cannot learn another heritage's magic, with two exceptions. Mixed heritage characters may use either parent's Heritage magic, as described below. Contract Magic is a circle magic, and anyone may learn it, though devils are the only people born with it. Like every circle magic, it can only be written outside combat.

      Mixed Heritage

      Terrana is old enough, and Monsera crowded enough, that plenty of people have parents of two different heritages. A mixed heritage character chooses one parent's heritage for their Innate Attribute, Innate Flaw, and Heritage Merit. For their free Heritage magic dot, they may choose the Heritage magic of either parent. Only one bloodline awakens. The other stays quiet.

      Fey Touched

      A character with one Fey or devil parent and one parent of any other heritage is Fey Touched. They follow the Mixed Heritage rules above, with one addition: a Fey Touched character lives about 400 years, whatever their other parent's lifespan. A Fey Touched Hyuman can expect to see four centuries. Devil Spawn are Fey Touched.

      Healing

      All healing in Terrana, magical or otherwise, works in tiers. Each success on a healing roll does one of the things its tier allows, chosen by the healer. Slaking a box lowers it one damage type (Aggravated to Lethal, or Lethal to Bashing). Neutralizing a box removes it entirely.

      Basic healing: Each success neutralizes 1 Bashing, or slakes 1 Lethal to Bashing. Basic healing cannot affect Aggravated damage.

      Advanced healing: Each success neutralizes 2 Bashing, neutralizes 1 Lethal, or slakes 1 Aggravated to Lethal.

      Expert healing: Each success neutralizes 4 Bashing, neutralizes 2 Lethal, or neutralizes 1 Aggravated.

      Master healing: Regrows lost limbs. Master healing can never be used in combat; the incantation takes several turns to complete. It is rare. Automaton prosthetics are common across Terrana, and some people would rather replace a lost limb with a stronger one than regrow it.

      Resurrection: The only healing above Master. It has its own chapter.

      Aggravated damage healed during the same combat in which it was taken usually leaves no scar, and that healing can even reattach a severed limb that is still on hand.

      Falling Damage

      A creature that falls rolls 1d10 for every 10 feet it falls. One zone straight up or down is about 10 feet. Falling damage is not a success roll: add the numbers shown on the dice, and that total is the damage. If the total is lower than the creature's BDT, it takes no damage. Otherwise it takes the damage like any other hit.

      Beastfolk

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      The Beastfolk are native to the Great Forest on the Milis Continent. Individuals live in Monsera, Breakwater, and other cities, but in small numbers. The exception is the Holy Kingdom of Milishion on the forest's edge, where Beastfolk make up about a quarter of the population. No other nation comes close.

      Their settlements are built high in the trees. These are full communities with streets, gathering places, and homes expanded over generations. Contact with Monsera has brought better lighting, crystal relay access, and improved building materials. The Beastfolk adopted what was useful and declined the rest.

      Each tribe has its own chief, and all chiefs answer to a head chief. By law and tradition, the head chief must come from a canine or feline bloodline. The Doldia family has held the position for generations. The other chiefs can jointly demand that the head chief step down, and the head chief may demand a formal challenge before stepping aside.

      Tribes differ in temperament. Some settle disputes quickly and loudly. Others are slow to anger and slow to forgive. All share a strong territorial sense. They protect the forest out of obligation, not hostility toward outsiders.

      Breeding seasons vary by animal type and are treated as a private matter. Rabbit Beastfolk are the only type broadly known to seek companionship outside their season. I include this because people ask, and an accurate answer is better than a guess.

      Splashing water on a Beastfolk person is one of the worst insults in their culture, and the response is usually physical. Visitors should know this before it happens by accident.

      Bestial Demon

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      The first time I met a Bestial on the Demon Continent, I mistook them for a monster. Most first time visitors make this mistake. Correcting it quickly is better for one's social standing and physical safety.

      Bestials walk upright, speak, and live in communities with their own traditions. Their animal heritage shows far more than it does in Beastfolk. A Bestial may have the full face of a boar, scaled hide from neck to toe, or a turtle's shell built into the skeleton.

      They are by far the largest population on the Demon Continent and have been throughout its long recorded history. Political power rests with the Immortal Demon Kings, whose domains have been stable for centuries. Bestials hold little of that power, but in practice they are equal citizens.

      Some types have well known names. Orcs are porcine: large, direct, and very serious about food. Kobolds are canine: quick, social, and loyal. Tortles are turtle-like: slow, deliberate, and patient enough to make Ogres look impulsive. Reptaurs are lizard-like and cold blooded, so their energy depends on the temperature.       Demon

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      The Demon Continent holds more peoples than any survey could count. Every valley, bog, and mountain pass seems to have its own tribe, and no two look alike.       Heritage Magic: Tribal Magic

      Every demon tribe carries its own magic in the blood. A Demon's Heritage magic is designed by the player and the Storyteller together around the tribe's nature, and it follows every rule for Heritage magic in this chapter. Use these guidelines when building it.

      Name: Give the magic a name that fits the tribe.

      Pools: Choose a Primary and a Secondary dice pool, each Attribute + Ability + your Heritage dots, like every other Heritage magic.

      Ranks: Seven ranks, Core through Cosmic. Each rank offers an Option A and an Option B spell, and the choice is permanent.

      Mana: Every spell costs mana equal to its rank. Upkeep is always the cost minus 1, so no Core spell can be persistent.

      Balance: Use the other Heritage magics in this chapter as a measuring stick. Rank 1 spells have no lasting effects in combat, and power should rise evenly from rank to rank.

      Pacing: The Storyteller may write the spells one rank at a time, as the character awakens each new dot, rather than all seven at creation.

      Devils

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      I have a personal stake in this entry. I was one of the first scholars outside Monsera to write about the devils settling on Floor 15 of The Dungeon. That article drew more correspondence than anything else I have published, and most of it came from people who had never met a devil.

      Devils are widely misunderstood, and the difficulty is that the common beliefs are not entirely false. They do harvest mana from the emotions of those around them. Hell has been a place of real suffering. What the reputation leaves out is that a people is not a single person, and the history of some devils does not predict the behavior of the one in front of you.

      Devils can regenerate mana from any emotion, not only suffering. The Immortal Devil Queen Sciel credits this discovery to her conversations with the Slime King about sixty years ago. If a devil can live on joy as easily as misery, there is no longer any reason to cause misery. Not all devils accept this. Zagan controls four levels of Hell and holds the Duchess Gremory prisoner. Marchosias fights beside him by choice.

      Most of the devils who reject Zagan's view now live in Nite City on Floor 15 of The Dungeon. It is a city where nearly everything is legal and cheap, where currency is often emotional rather than financial, and where seven districts correspond to the seven emotional affinities.

      Sciel was Nite City's first mayor and held the post until the city could govern itself. Since then she has traveled as an envoy with the Slime King's authority, arguing that devils deserve to exist without proving it every day. She writes books and appears on Crystalz programs. We exchange manuscripts before publication, and her notes on mine are always correct.

      Hell has nine levels. Level 9 is the entry point for the banished. Level 1 is the deepest, where Lucifer holds court. Each level is ruled by a prince, princess, duke, or duchess. Zagan has been conquering levels and claiming them for himself. Lucifer has responded with restraint. He cares for the devils, Hyumans, and others in Hell in his own way, and his anti authoritarian philosophy makes simply ordering Zagan to stop feel inconsistent to him. Hell is at a stalemate.

      Belethore, Lucifer's son and Sciel's former betrothed, was killed and reincarnated into an ordinary devil family with his memories and most of his power intact. He now goes by Mahk and recently took over Level 14 3/4 Studios, between Floors 14 and 15 of The Dungeon. His shows feature devils alongside people of every heritage, slowly making them ordinary in the public eye.

      I am often asked whether devils can be trusted. It is not a question anyone asks about Elves or Dwarves, and it assumes a uniformity that does not exist. The devils I know have been exactly as trustworthy as everyone else I know. It depends on the individual.

      A devil's skin color shows their emotional affinity. The Aceditia (Sloth and Diligence) are blue and gray. The Iracunditia (Wrath and Patience) are deep red and black. The Gulanitia (Gluttony and Temperance) are amber and burnt orange. The Vanitia (Pride and Humility) are bronze and copper. The Inviditia (Envy and Kindness) are green and teal. The Carnavitia (Lust and Chastity) are violet and soft pink. The Avaritia (Greed and Charity) are gold and alabaster. Immortal devils show a single solid color. Their descendants carry both colors of their type in a pattern unique to each individual. Skin color tells you a devil's affinity. It does not tell you their character.

      Dragonfolk

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      I have never met a pure blooded Dragonfolk. I have met several of mixed heritage, corresponded with two more, and read everything the Monsera University library holds on the subject, which is not much. The pure blooded do not oppose scholarship. They decline to be its subject.

      What is broadly understood about the pure blooded is this: they are genderless, hatched from eggs, androgynous in appearance, and fixed in number. How the population stays fixed is a private matter involving soul reincarnation, and they do not discuss it. Asking will not get an answer.

      Fishfolk

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      The Fishfolk are among the most important peoples in Terrana, and most of Terrana does not know it. For most of recorded history they have done work of great consequence without recognition, and they appear content with that.

      Vampire

      From "Peoples of the Known World" by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

      The vampires of the Shaded Isles noble house are currently the most visible of their kind, a result of the isles joining the Monsera kingdom. I have met several members of the royal family, though not the queen.

      Vampires are sustained by mana, which places them among the Corporeal Undead without making them identical to the others. They do not age while their mana is maintained. They can regenerate mana from emotion as devils do, and they can drink blood, which is their most efficient source. They drink openly and without embarrassment, and the wider world accepts it as a known vampire trait.

      Sunlight does not destroy them. It drains their mana faster and causes discomfort, so most avoid long exposure. In the Shaded Isles the permanent overcast makes the question moot. Elsewhere they manage it as a known limitation.

      A vampire colony exists in the far north of the Central Continent, where the sun is absent for much of the year. I have not visited. Travelers describe an insular community, not hostile but not welcoming, that trades occasionally with nearby Hyuman settlements and is otherwise left alone.

      Every vampire has a compulsion tied to their strongest Virtue or Vice. When triggered, it demands to be satisfied, and resisting it is possible but costly. I have met vampires compelled toward acquisition, toward self sabotage, and toward fixation on a single task. None discuss it casually, and I did not press. Those who spoke of it described living with it rather than having overcome it.

      Vampires are born or turned. Born vampires grow up with their nature and treat it as a plain fact. Turned vampires come to it through some irreversible event later in life and tend to be more reflective about it, since they remember being something else.

      Vampire - Mechanical Block

      Average Lifespan: Immortal. Sustained by mana. Does not age while mana is maintained.

      Innate Attribute: Any single attribute of the player's choice. Vampires are as varied in natural ability as Hyumans. That attribute starts at 2 dots and its maximum is raised by 1 beyond the column cap. Note Innate on sheet.

      Innate Flaw - Compulsion: Every vampire has a compulsion derived from one of their Virtue or Vice extremes. When triggered the vampire must act on their compulsion or take 1 Bashing damage per turn in combat or 1 Bashing damage per scene out of combat until they give in or the trigger is removed. Consult the compulsion table below and work with your Storyteller to determine what specifically triggers yours.

      Attribute Flaw: None. Like Hyumans, Vampires may place any attribute group in their Affinity column.

      Vampiric Compulsion Scale
      • Humility: Self Sabotage - compelled to undermine their own success or status in a meaningful way
      • Pride: Dominance Display - compelled to publicly establish superiority over whoever they perceive as challenging them
      • Charity: Ruinous Giving - compelled to give away something of genuine value, money, information, or an advantage they cannot afford to lose
      • Greed: Acquisition - compelled to take something they want whether it belongs to someone else or not
      • Chastity: Denial - compelled to deny themselves something they genuinely want, a pleasure, an indulgence, or a desire they have been holding back
      • Lust: Fixation - compelled to pursue whoever has captured their attention and make moves to claim them as their own regardless of consequences
      • Patience: Paralysis - compelled to wait and do nothing even when action is desperately needed
      • Wrath: Blood Rage - compelled to physically harm whoever triggered their anger
      • Temperance: Abstinence - compelled to refuse all nourishment including blood and mana restoration until the feeling passes
      • Gluttony: Feeding Frenzy - compelled to feed, blood or emotion, from whoever is nearest regardless of consent or consequence
      • Kindness: Bleeding Heart - compelled to help whoever appears to be suffering even if doing so puts themselves or their allies in danger
      • Envy: Sabotage - compelled to undermine or damage something belonging to whoever they envy
      • Diligence: Obsessive Focus - compelled to complete whatever task is in front of them to the exclusion of everything else including immediate threats
      • Sloth: Shutdown - compelled to stop moving, acting, or engaging entirely until the feeling passes

      A Note on Turned Vampires: A character turned into a vampire through play gains the Compulsion flaw and functional immortality. They do not receive the Innate Attribute benefit or a free dot in a Vampiric Power. Vampiric Powers may be purchased with XP like any other Heritage magic.
