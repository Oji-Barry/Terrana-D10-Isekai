Introduction

One hundred floors down, and every one of them is open for business.

From “A Survey of the Known World” by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

Most dungeons in Terrana are dangerous accidents of mana and time. The Grand Dungeon is dangerous on purpose. It sits just east of Monsera's capital beneath the great colosseum, and it is the only dungeon I know of with a staff roster, a payroll, and a complaints department.

I have been as far as the seventy-fifth floor. I went no further, and I do not regret it.

The Grand Dungeon spans exactly one hundred floors. It is co-owned by the Slime King and the Earth God, a fairy-type Immortal Demon King who serves as its creative director. She redesigns floor layouts regularly so that veteran delvers never grow too comfortable. Because she has no interest in day-to-day logistics, a large company runs the dungeon's daily operations under her supervision.

No adventuring party has ever cleared the final ten floors.

How the Dungeon Works

Rules of Entry

Access to the dungeon is strictly regulated. The entry turnstiles will not turn for anyone without a dungeon bracelet. The bracelets do not belong to any guild. They are issued and regulated by the Arena above the dungeon, which is owned by the Kingdom of Monsera, so no single guild is responsible for keeping the dungeon's rules.

The Shape of the Dungeon

Every ten floors form one stretch with two biomes. The first biome runs from the first floor of the stretch to the fourth, and its last floor ends at a mini-boss who must be defeated before delvers can enter the safe zone on the fifth floor. The second biome runs from the sixth floor to the tenth, where a boss guards the way down.

The floors grow smaller the deeper you go, but they never become small. The upper floors hold open fields and whole cities, and even the deepest floors are wide enough to hold landscapes of their own. The hundredth floor is a single massive arena.

The one exception is the final stretch. Floors 91 through 100 form a single biome with no safe zone on the ninety-fifth floor.

Safe Zones and Fast Travel

Every floor ending in five is a safe zone and a permanent community. Reaching a safe zone for the first time unlocks its teleporter for you. Teleporters connect only safe zones, so fast travel is possible only between them. Every safe zone enforces the same three laws, kept by Golem Guards:

1. No bloodshed.

2. No theft.

3. No harmful spells.

Breaking any of these laws results in immediate punishment. Nite City, on the fifteenth floor, is the one place where the laws bend; see its entry.

Dungeon Bracelets and Respawn Lockers

Every delver is assigned a respawn locker on the surface, and their bracelet holds a mana crystal linked to that locker. The bracelet works only in the Grand Dungeon. It does nothing anywhere else in Terrana, and no other dungeon has anything like it.

As long as they wear a bracelet, no one can die in the Grand Dungeon. When a character is Incapacitated and no one heals them back up on the very next turn, their body vanishes. The bracelet consumes its mana crystal and teleports them to their locker on the surface. Because of this, no one in the dungeon can ever choose Blaze of Glory or Risk It.

Respawning does not fully heal a character. Remove all of their Lethal and Aggravated damage, so they never have to worry about a missing limb or a huge scar, then mark every Bashing box, even if their Bashing track was not full when they fell. A character who cannot heal will be in pain for a while, but a night of rest usually sets them right.

Their personal teleport destination also resets to the nearest safe zone above the floor where they fell.

Ecosystem and Economy

The Inhabitants: The monsters roaming the floors are paid employees of the dungeon company. When defeated, they respawn and return to their living quarters on the nearest safe zone.

The Loot: The company buys the treasures placed throughout the floors. Over time, the high ambient mana inside the dungeon gives these well-made items unpredictable, random magical properties.

Floors 1 to 10

Floors 1 to 4: The Upper Halls

Wide stone corridors lit by mana lamps, home to slimes, goblins, kobolds, and giant rats. These floors are where every delver learns the basics, and they are always busy with new guild members on their first runs.

Warchief Gruk Ironmaw

Mini-Boss, Floor 4

A veteran goblin warchief and a dungeon employee of thirty years, Gruk leads a warband that has humbled more fresh parties than any other foe in the dungeon. He fights with a crude cleaver and a lot of shouting, and he respects any party that beats him fairly.

Floor 5: Falsky

Safe Zone

Falsky is the largest floor in the dungeon. A false sky enchantment projects clouds, birds, and warm golden light over rolling fields of grass and wheat, scattered farmsteads, and a market village at its heart.

The whole community is built to look the way the world did before the Slime King arrived. Its residents prefer the old ways: shopkeepers, tavern keepers, and farmers dress in the older styles, trade by hand, and keep modern devices out of sight. Some live this way because they love it. Plenty of others work here in costume and go home to the surface at the end of their shift to live ordinary modern lives.

Floors 6 to 9: The Fungal Grotto

Damp caverns lit by glowing mushrooms the size of houses. Spore clouds, myconid scouts, and cave crawlers make these floors the first real test of a party's endurance.

The Grand Beastmaster

Boss, Floor 10: The Ironwood Colosseum

An open-air stone amphitheater ringed by ironwood trees, its stands packed with off-duty dungeon staff placing bets. The Grand Beastmaster, a high-ranking Orc commander from the Laborers Guild, fights alongside a huge multi-headed chimera. The fight is loud and theatrical, and it tests whether a party can work together before going deeper.

Floors 11 to 20

Floors 11 to 14: The Great Labyrinth

From the moment a party leaves the Colosseum, it enters a single labyrinth that runs all the way to the gates of Nite City. It is difficult to tell which floor you have reached, and entire parties have starved until their bracelets sent them home. Adventurers use a site on the CCN to try to map it, but the Earth God changes the layout so often that a complete map may be impossible.

Asterion of the Maze

Mini-Boss, Floor 14

A minotaur who has walked the Labyrinth longer than any map has existed. Asterion hunts by sound and smell, and he is waiting at the Labyrinth's final gate for anyone who makes it that far.

Floor 14¾: The Studios

Hidden in a pocket between Floors 14 and 15, this secret level is the main content production hub for the CCN. It is run by an unusual pair: Mahk the Boiler, whose true name is Belethore and who once ruled the second layer of Hell, and Hea'vee Starve'aey, a Sphinx game show host.

Floor 15: Sciel City (Nite City)

Safe Zone

Officially Sciel City, this floor is known to nearly every adventurer as Nite City. It looks like Tokyo, New York, and Las Vegas thrown together, and everything here is cheap and entirely legal. More people live in Nite City than on any other floor, though Falsky covers more ground.

Nite City was founded about sixty years ago with the Slime King's help. The Immortal Devil Queen Sciel, once ruler of Hell's third layer, had grown tired of Hell's economy, which runs on mana harvested from suffering. She discovered that devils can harvest and regenerate mana just as well from happy, positive emotions, and she left Hell before its war truly began. Since then the city has been a refuge for devils fleeing the underworld. Every devil living here is under contract to the Slime King and the Earth God.

Nite City is the one safe zone where the three laws bend. The devils mostly regulate their own rules here, and the laws are broken more often than anywhere else in the dungeon. It is a wild place, but the locals still frown on anyone fighting openly unless someone is betting on the fight or paying for it. You will hear “gold is king” on every corner, but it is a saying, not a law.

The city is governed by a parliament led by an executive Mayor and seven District Heads. Each district is tied to one of the seven scales of virtue and vice.

Floors 16 to 19: The Gearworks

Below Nite City the stone gives way to brass. Catwalks hang over grinding gears and hissing steam lines, and clockwork guardians patrol on fixed routes. Timing matters more than strength here.

The Chrono-Goliath

Boss, Floor 20: The Shattered Clockwork

A huge, ancient steam automaton built by the Makers Guild, standing on a brass platform above a sea of gears. It changes the speed of the floor's machinery as it fights, forcing parties to time their movements around shifting platforms and bursting steam pipes while they work to crack its armor.

Floors 21 to 30

Floors 21 to 24: The Canopy Wilds

A rainforest under artificial sunlight, crossed high above the forest floor on branches and vines. Giant insects, gliding predators, and territorial birds make every crossing a risk.

Skyrend, the Storm Alpha

Mini-Boss, Floor 24

The great thunderbird that rules the canopy. Skyrend dives out of the artificial clouds trailing lightning and knocks careless delvers from the branches.

Floor 25: Canopy Station

Safe Zone

Hollowed-out redwoods serve as homes and shops, connected by suspension bridges and wooden walkways. A few thousand people live here year-round. It is the main source of fresh alchemy ingredients and monster-taming supplies in the upper dungeon, and the local Adventurers Guild outpost posts monster-tracking bounties for the surrounding floors.

Floors 26 to 29: The Flooded Caverns

The forest drains into black water. These floors are half-submerged tunnels and drowned halls, where delvers wade, swim, and hold their breath past eels, drowned guardians, and things that wait below the surface.

The Kraken Vanguard

Boss, Floor 30: The Coral Throne

A deep underwater amphitheater ringed by sharp reefs and sunken galleons, swept by currents strong enough to drag a delver across the floor. The Kraken Vanguard, a huge and intelligent Seafolk champion under contract with the Navigators Guild, fights with sweeping tentacles, ink clouds that blind casters, and whirlpools that split a party apart.

Floors 31 to 40

Floors 31 to 34: The Forge Depths

Mine shafts and smelting halls, hot and loud, where ore carts run on their own and golems shamble through the smoke. The heat wears on anyone who lingers.

The Iron Juggernaut

Mini-Boss, Floor 34

A walking furnace of riveted plates, built long ago to clear cave-ins and later put to work guarding the way to the Foundry. It is slow, nearly unstoppable, and very hard to hurt from the front.

Floor 35: Neo Foundry

Safe Zone

A retro-industrial dwarven smithy crossed with a modern assembly line, full of automated steam hammers, molten brass channels, and neon schematics projected on soot-stained stone. Its population is mostly smiths and their families, a working town of a couple of thousand. It is the first safe zone with high-grade weapon modification and armor rebuilding, and Makers Guild workshops repair broken gear or melt down scrap loot into raw materials.

Floors 36 to 39: The Hall of Illusions

Endless galleries and painted halls where doors lead back to where you started, portraits watch you pass, and nothing is quite where it looks to be. Trusting your eyes is the most common mistake on these floors.

The Doppler Matriarch

Boss, Floor 40: The Mirror Gallery

An eight-sided ballroom where every wall, floor, and ceiling tile is an unbreakable silver mirror. The Doppler Matriarch, a high-tier devil illusionist from Nite City, creates solid mirror copies of each party member with their exact abilities, and the party must work out which reflection is the real one controlling the rest.

Floors 41 to 50

Floors 41 to 44: The Tide Ruins

Ruined seaside temples in a vast cavern where an indoor tide rises and falls on a schedule. Delvers have to read the tide tables or be cut off as the water comes in.

Mother Maelis, the Siren Matron

Mini-Boss, Floor 44

The eldest of the ruins' sirens. Her song draws delvers into the rising water, and she is far more dangerous to a party that cannot stop listening.

Floor 45: Sunken Boardwalk

Safe Zone

A seaside pier inside a water-filled cavern, lined with carnival games, neon taverns, and seafood shacks looking out over a glowing indoor ocean with moving tides. About a thousand people live here, many of them Seafolk. The Navigators Guild runs this rest stop, where delvers can hire aquatic guides, buy underwater breathing gear, or gamble their coin at the boardwalk games.

Floors 46 to 49: The Volcanic Fields

The water ends at fields of cooling lava, ash storms, and steam vents. Fire elementals and salamanders thrive here, and the ground itself is not always safe to stand on.

Cindermaw, the Magma Wyrm

Boss, Floor 50: The Ash Refinery

A circular platform surrounded by pools of magma and roaring smoke extractors, where the heat alone wears delvers down. Cindermaw is a wild fire drake fused with experimental company machinery. It melts parts of the arena as the fight goes on, shrinking the ground a party has left to stand on.

Floors 51 to 60

Floors 51 to 54: The Glyph Halls

Halls of dark volcanic rock carved with glowing glyphs. Many doors open only for those who can read them, and the wrong answer triggers wards and traps. Scholars do well here.

The Rune Sentinel

Mini-Boss, Floor 54

An ancient guardian covered in shifting runes, posed in front of the Archives' gate. It asks questions before it fights, and parties that answer well find it easier to beat.

Floor 55: Archives of Slate

Safe Zone

A quiet library carved from volcanic rock, where thousands of slate tablets fill towering shelves that slide on brass tracks. Only a few hundred people live here, almost all of them researchers and archivists. The Scholars Guild runs it, and it is a required stop for identifying high-level artifacts or translating coded scripts found below. Delvers can also buy rare spell scrolls or trade historical findings for dungeon credit.

Floors 56 to 59: The Catacombs

Narrow burial tunnels stacked with crumbling sarcophagi and choked with dust. Undead patrol the dark, and the tight corridors make it hard for ranged fighters to find a clear shot.

The Dullahan Overseer

Boss, Floor 60: The Silent Necropolis

A sprawling mausoleum of tight corridors. The Dullahan Overseer, an ancient armored knight representing the noble houses of the Shaded Isles, steps through the shadowed walls to cut off retreat and lets out deadly wails that test a party's mental grit and physical endurance.

Floors 61 to 70

Floors 61 to 64: The Old Rail Tunnels

Abandoned service tunnels from an older version of the dungeon's transit system. Derelict cars, live rails, and scavenging constructs fill the dark, and the occasional train still comes through without warning.

Conductor Kessler

Mini-Boss, Floor 64

The ghostly conductor of a train that was decommissioned long ago and never stopped running. Kessler fights from his engine and insists that every passenger present a valid ticket.

Floor 65: Iron Terminus

Safe Zone

A crowded Victorian-style subway terminal of black iron and white tile, loud with humming teleporters and commuter trains carrying dungeon staff. Only a couple of hundred people live here; most of the crowd is passing through. It is the main transit station for the dungeon's monster employees. Delvers can visit the Healers Guild clinic for advanced curse removal, use secure bank vaults, or take delivery jobs to the lower floors.

Floors 66 to 69: The Arcane Laboratories

Sealed research wings full of failed experiments, unstable spell circuits, and escaped test subjects. The magic here is volatile, and spells sometimes behave in ways their casters did not intend.

Warden Arcanum

Boss, Floor 70: The Circuit Vault

A sealed security hall lined with glowing mana circuits, where the wrong floor tile sets off a burst of lightning. Warden Arcanum, an advanced defense construct built by the Mages Guild, absorbs elemental spells to power its own devastating attacks. Parties must rely on physical strikes and non-magical tricks to shut down its power cores.

Floors 71 to 80

Floors 71 to 74: The Crystal Mines

Tunnels lined with raw mana crystal that hums and glows. The crystal is valuable and fragile, and so are the creatures made of it.

The Crystal Basilisk

Mini-Boss, Floor 74

A great lizard whose hide is a living crystal lattice. Its gaze turns flesh to crystal, and it guards the last approach to the Obsidian Vault.

Floor 75: Obsidian Vault

Safe Zone

Sleek corporate minimalism in seamless black obsidian, lit by blue ley-line circuitry. There are no markets here, only a few elite offices run by the Merchants Guild and staffed by a few dozen people. The Vault deals only in gold-tier transactions, rare mana crystal trades, and luxury gear for veteran parties.

Floors 76 to 79: The Thinning Halls

No one would be allowed to keep a real Void breach open, so these floors are built to look like one. Gravity shifts, rooms repeat, and constructs modeled after real, carefully studied voidlings stalk the halls. Some of the voidlings here are not constructs at all: the company captures real ones and keeps them contained on these floors.

The Rift Stalker

Boss, Floor 80: The Void Fissure

A narrow, unstable ledge above a convincing replica of a tear in the Void, where howling winds pull at anyone standing too close to the edge. The Rift Stalker is the real thing: a captured multi-dimensional predator, held on this floor by magic barriers that keep it from escaping for everyone's safety. It phases out of reality between its turns and leaves gravity anomalies behind, forcing delvers to balance attacking with keeping their footing.

Floors 81 to 90

Floors 81 to 84: The Temple Ascent

A mountain temple path of endless stone stairs, shrines, and narrow ledges. Gravity is reversed on these floors, which is how delvers climb the stairs while still traveling downward into the dungeon. The monks who guard it test discipline as much as strength.

The Monk of a Thousand Steps

Mini-Boss, Floor 84

A silent Ogre who waits at the top of the final stair. He fights unarmed, never seems to tire, and will not let a party pass until they prove themselves worthy.

Floor 85: Sage's Solitude

Safe Zone

The quietest safe zone in the dungeon: a walled garden of white stone on a high mountain floor, holding a single cherry blossom tree whose petals never pile up, raked white gravel, and natural hot springs with healing qualities. Only a handful of keepers live here. There are no shops or bars, only the springs themselves, which heal the body and are dense enough with mana to fully replenish a caster's mana. It is the last place to rest before the final floors, and the last teleporter in the dungeon.

Floors 86 to 89: The Infernal Halls

Halls of brimstone and chains, staffed by devils on contract who did not choose Nite City's gentler life. The air is hot and heavy, and the fighting is brutal.

The Devil Prince Defiler

Boss, Floor 90: The Executioner's Cage

A hanging iron cage in total darkness with nowhere to hide and no room to run. The Devil Prince Defiler, an exile from the Hell Dungeon under personal contract to the Earth God, is a true spellsword. Like most devils he is strongest with magic, hurling hellfire and curses from range, but he is just as dangerous up close with brutal melee combinations. Equally deadly at any distance, he is a pure test of how well a party can take damage and deal it back.

Floors 91 to 100

Floors 91 to 99: The Deep

The final stretch is a single descent through the dragon's domain: ancient halls scorched by old fire, piles of bones and treasure, and guardians who have served the same master for centuries. There is no safe zone on the ninety-fifth floor and no teleporter anywhere below Sage's Solitude, so a party must make the whole descent in one push. This is a key reason no party has ever cleared the final floors.

Vaelthrax

Final Boss, Floor 100: The Last Lair

The entire hundredth floor is one massive arena of ancient stone, the largest battleground in the dungeon, its edges heaped with glowing mana crystals and historic artifacts. Vaelthrax is an ancient dragon and the Slime King's legendary, undefeated companion. The fight has no gimmicks or environmental tricks: only immense physical strength, near-total immunity to magic, and devastating breath. It is the final exam for anyone who has survived the descent.

Storyteller Note: The Dragon Above

Vaelthrax does not spend all his time in his lair. He can take a Hyuman-looking form, and in that form he lives a separate life on the surface under the name Thraxus. He is not a very creative dragon. Very few people know who he really is, and a character could meet him in Monsera without ever realizing it.
