Overview

Older generations call them Blessings and Curses; modern science calls them Merits and Flaws. Both are understood to be the same ambient magic that touches every newborn, simply with different outcomes. A Merit is something the world handed you that helps. A Flaw is something the world handed you that does not. Neither was asked for, and neither can be bought or removed with XP after character creation.

At character creation you have 7 points to spend on Merits. You may take up to 7 points of Flaws, and each Flaw point adds 1 point to your Merit budget, for a possible total of 14. Any points left unspent carry over to your Backgrounds in Step 8.

Your Heritage Merit from Chapter 8 is recorded here too, but it is free and does not use any of these points.

Because Merits and Flaws never change with XP, it helps to fill your Merits, Flaws, and Backgrounds section from the bottom up for Merits and Flaws, and from the top down for Backgrounds. That keeps the two visually apart and easy to read at a glance.

Adjustments for Attributes and Abilities

Before spending any Merit points, consider whether your character needs to go beyond the normal maximums, or would happily accept a weakness in exchange for strength elsewhere.

You may raise or lower the maximum of any single Attribute, or of any Ability group, by spending or recouping Merit points. The cost or refund is always the larger of the two numbers involved.

Raising: Raising a maximum by 1 costs points equal to the new maximum. Raising Intelligence from a maximum of 5 to 6 costs 6 points. Raising it again to 7 costs 7 more, for 13 in total. This is expensive on purpose.

Lowering: Lowering a maximum by 1 refunds points equal to the current maximum. Lowering Manipulation from a maximum of 4 to 3 refunds 4 points.

Ability groups: The same rules apply. Lowering a Noble group valued at 2 down to a maximum of 1 refunds 2 points. Raising a Mage group valued at 5 to a maximum of 6 costs 6.

Adjustments only change what a trait can reach later through XP. They do not change how many dots you spend at creation, and the Golden Rule still stops anything from being filled above 5 during creation.

Note raised maximums as Max Boost 1, Max Boost 2, and so on next to the Attribute, and lowered maximums as Max Loss 1, Max Loss 2. For an Ability group, change the number written next to the group. Write a short note explaining each adjustment so you and your Storyteller can track them.

Example: The Sword Mage

A player wants a sword mage, equally dangerous with blade and spell. They make Physical their Affinity column (6) and Mental their Standard column (5), so Intelligence can only reach 5. They want it to reach 7. Raising to 6 costs 6 and raising to 7 costs 7 more: 13 points, more than their budget of 7.

Social is their Aversion column (4), and Manipulation does not matter to this character, so they lower its maximum from 4 to 3 and recoup 4 points. They also lower the Noble group from 2 to 1 and recoup 2 more. That gives them 6 extra points, which with their starting 7 makes exactly 13. They note Max Boost 2 next to Intelligence and Max Loss 1 next to Manipulation, and change the Noble group's number from 2 to 1.

They still spend 5 points in the Mental column and 2 dots in the Noble group at creation, and Intelligence still cannot be filled above 5 until play begins. After that, it can grow all the way to 7.

Magic Eyes

Empress Kishirika Kishirisu is famous for gifting her Magic Eyes to those who earn her favor. A character can begin play with one through the Magic Eye merit, or receive one from Kishirika during the story. A Magic Eye replaces one of your eyes and can be opened or closed at will. While open it is attuned: it holds 1 mana committed against your Maximum Mana Per Turn. Closing it returns that mana in your next Upkeep Phase.

Foresight, Absorption, All-Seeing, and Absolute Void are greater eyes and cost 7 points. The rest cost 5.

Fey Touched

A character with one Fey or devil parent and one parent of any other heritage is Fey Touched (Chapter 8). They live about 400 years whatever their other parent's lifespan, and they may carry a little of the otherworld in them. Each Gift costs 3 points, up to 2 Gifts.

Wings: You have wings and fly at double your normal movement. Record a free specialty in Athletics called Flight.

Second Bloodline: You may raise your other parent's Heritage magic with XP, starting at 0 dots, following all the normal Heritage magic rules.

Fey Sight: You can see fey, spirits, and glamours that are hidden from ordinary eyes.

Fey Charm: Add a bonus die to Expression and Performance rolls meant to enchant or enthrall an audience.

Otherworldly Look: A feature of your choice marks your heritage: horns, a tail, unusual eyes, or something stranger. Add a bonus die to Presence rolls to unsettle or impress.

Curses

Choose one of these curses or design your own with the Storyteller. They come in three tiers: an inconvenience (2 or 3 points), a burden (4 or 5 points), and a curse that defines your life (6 or 7 points). Any curse can be broken in play, usually as the goal of a story.

Hex of Small Misfortunes (2 points): Once per session, the Storyteller can turn one of your successful rolls on a minor task outside combat into a failure. It can only cause a small inconvenience, such as a dropped key, a snapped bootstring, or ink spilled on a letter, never real harm or the loss of a scene.

Beast's Dread (3 points): Animals hate you. You take a penalty die on Ride and on any roll to calm, train, or bond with a creature, and mounts throw you when startled.

Ill-Starred (4 points): The first Fate point you spend each session does nothing.

Withering Flesh (5 points): Healing magic and potions heal 1 less box on you. Wounds that heal naturally leave scars people notice.

The Thing That Follows (6 points): Something bound to the curse, such as a spirit, a voidling, or a dead rival, keeps finding you. The Storyteller brings it into every story arc.

Creeping Doom (7 points): The curse is slowly winning. The Storyteller marks a tally each session. When it fills, something terrible happens, such as a death, a transformation, or a great loss, unless the curse is broken first.

Special Powers

Some characters arrive with something that fits no standard category: a power so specific and so significant that it defines them. These are the big ones, the potentially game-changing ones, borrowed from other stories, other worlds, or invented from scratch.

Special Powers cost 7 points per dot, with a maximum of 2 dots at creation (14 points). Whether more dots can be bought with XP later is entirely up to the Storyteller. Storyteller approval is required for everything in this section, and the Storyteller may change costs, add conditions, or say no if a power does not fit the campaign.

A power this big rarely comes without strings. Consider pairing it with the Devil's Bargain flaw.

Devil's Bargains

Choose one of these bargains or design your own with the Storyteller. Each is a debt, a limit, or a weakness. 1 dot gives 3 points back, 2 dots give 5, and 3 dots give 7. A bargain can be paid off or broken in play, but whoever holds the contract wrote it carefully.

One Favor Owed (1 dot, 3 points): Once per story arc, your patron can call in a favor. Until you do it, your power does not work.

The Price of Use (1 dot, 3 points): Each use of the power costs 1 Willpower.

The Tithe (2 dots, 5 points): Your patron feeds on you. Your Mana Pool is 3 lower.

The Bane (2 dots, 5 points): Choose one thing, such as silver, holy ground, running water, or a spoken name. While you touch it or stand in it, the power fails and you take a penalty die on everything.

Soul Collateral (3 dots, 7 points): Your soul belongs to your patron. If you die, you cannot be Resurrected without the patron's consent, and the patron always names a price.

The Contract Comes Due (3 dots, 7 points): The bargain has an end date set with your Storyteller. When it arrives, you pay in full or lose the power forever, and the patron comes to collect.

Arcane Enhancements

Arcane Enhancements, or augmentation, are a special merit open to every character, but they are most common in the Corporate Kingdoms of Shirone, Kikka, and Sanika on the Central Continent, where back-alley augmentation shops make them easier to get than anywhere else in the world.

Each enhancement is its own merit with its own cost, recorded as a separate entry in your Merits section. You may take up to two enhancements at creation. More can be added later at the Storyteller's discretion. In the Corporate Kingdoms, part of the XP cost is replaced by in-game costs, such as jobs, debts, or trades with whoever performs the work, so the XP cost there is lower than anywhere else.

The body's magical channels have limits, and augmentation permanently takes up part of that capacity. Every enhancement lowers your Maximum Mana Per Turn by 1. Heavy enhancements lower it by 2.

A bonus dot from an enhancement is added on top of the trait. It does not change the trait's own dots, so the Golden Rule does not limit it, and it can take a trait past its normal cap. Note Arcane next to the trait on your sheet and write the bonus in its bonus space.
