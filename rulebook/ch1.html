Introduction

Terrana was not born as one world. It was forged from the ruins of six.

From “On the Nature of Worlds” by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

People often ask me what the world is. The short answer is that ours is not the only world that ever existed. It is the only one left.

There were six, once, joined around a seventh place at their center. Five were destroyed in the God Wars. Their survivors came here, to the Hyuman World, and brought their peoples, their magic, and their grudges with them. Terrana is what grew from that.

Some of what follows is recorded history. Some is theory from my colleagues at the university. Some is faith. I have tried to mark which is which, and where the record runs out, I say so.

The Six-Faced World

Before recorded history, six worlds existed together. Each was its own complete reality with its own peoples, cultures, and magic: the Hyuman World, the Beast World, the Demon World, the Dragon World, the Sky World, and the Sea World.

The six were joined like bubbles pressed around a smaller seventh at their center. That center was the Void. Potent magic held the structure together. The walls where the worlds met were flat, while every outer surface stayed round. Scholars call the whole structure the Six-Faced World.

The God Wars

The Hyuman God went missing. Something took his place: an impostor now called the Man God, who claimed his title and his authority.

The Man God murdered Lunaria, wife of the Dragon God and sister of the Hyuman God, and arranged for the gods of the other worlds to take the blame. In his grief, the Dragon God went to war. One by one, the Dragon World, the Demon World, the Beast World, the Sea World, and the Sky World were destroyed.

The Hyuman World survived because it sat on the far side of the structure, the hardest of the six to reach. When the fighting ended, the survivors of the five fallen worlds fled there. They brought their heritages, their traditions, their magic, and their grief. The Hyuman World took them all in, and it became Terrana.

The impostor did not escape what he started. The Man God, known by the name Hitogami, is bound in the Void, and he remains there today.

Some of this history is left uncertain on purpose. The Followers of Milis worship the Hyuman God as though he were the God of Creation, and the faithful and the scholars of Terrana do not always agree on what happened or why. Mysticism and faith have as much room in Terrana as the university's records do.

Terrana and the Void Today

Five of the six outer worlds are gone. What remains are two bubbles pressed flat against each other: Terrana on top and the Void World beneath it. The land of Terrana sits on the shared wall between them.

Mana passes freely back and forth through that wall. Most of the Void is formless creation energy, the source of all mana. It takes no shape until someone gives it one, which is what a mage does when casting a spell.

Monsera University's current theory holds that the Void contains two kinds of mana, and that magic is what happens when the two fuse. It is the best explanation anyone has offered so far, but it remains a theory.

The Dreeming and The Seeming

When Hitogami lost his hold on the Void, it split into regions. In two of them, belief takes shape. Stories, dreams, and fears that enough people believe strongly enough take form there, and a character believed in hard enough can appear as a living being.

The Dreeming: Where Terrana itself looks like a grounded anime world, the Dreeming is wonky and cartoonish, a place of crooked towers, oversized mushrooms, and landscapes that bend like the pages of an old children's comic. Kind and wondrous beliefs come to life here, and the fey who thrive in it are generally good-natured.

The Seeming: The Seeming is a nightmarish hellscape where fears and dark tales are made real. Its landscape is grotesque, and the things that thrive there are the self-serving ones.

The two must stay in balance, but the Seeming does not rest. Its corruption almost always works toward ways of taking over the Dreeming, and the border between them is under constant pressure.

Beyond them lies the rest of the Void, raw and formless. Somewhere within it, Hitogami remains bound.

The Heavens Above Terrana

Above Terrana's surface is a vast sky, and above the sky is space. Space holds other worlds, other peoples, and monarchies that rule whole regions of it. In the present day, the people of Terrana know none of this.

Games in Other Eras

This book's default setting is Terrana as it is today, but the system is built to carry a game into any era of the world's history.

The Distant Past: A game set long ago, during or just after the Great Hyuman-Demon War: a dark age of war, kingdoms, and swords.

A Fallen Future: A post-apocalyptic Terrana where today's modern technology has become a collection of ancient relics.

The Space Era: A researcher at Monsera discovers that the energy signal of the Rift matches energy coming from far out in the sky. The Hero Rangers Corps forms a sky division to travel to other planets and connect their peoples. Games in this era take place hundreds to a thousand years after that discovery.

In the Space Era, ships are common, though never cheap. Many planets and species are friendly to the Hero Rangers Corps, and some are not. Mercenary crews fly for hire under the Mercenaries Guild, much as adventurers work under the Adventurers Guild today, while space monarchies rule their own corners of the sky. Terrana itself remains playable throughout.

The closest anyone has come to the source of the Rift's signal is Nyxhollow, a planet full of the voidlings that sometimes break into Terrana. Its energy resembles the Rift's, but the signal leads much further, into the deepest reaches of space.

The full details of the Space Era and the other alternate eras are in Chapter 16.

Those Who Arrive

From “On Arriving” by Liris Kant, Third Chair of Natural Philosophy, Monsera University

Souls from other worlds sometimes arrive in Terrana. The scholarly term is displacement reincarnation. Nearly everyone else says isekai.

It has happened for as long as there are records. Most arrive as infants. Some arrive as children. A few arrive as adults and wake somewhere unfamiliar with no idea how they got there.

What they bring varies. Some bring memories of their old lives. Some bring knowledge of things this world had never seen. Some bring nothing but themselves.

The Slime King is the best-known case and, by general agreement, the most extreme. Monsera University's Special Students program exists to find and support others like him. In my experience, most of them settle in well.
