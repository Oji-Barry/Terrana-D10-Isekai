A Foreword from Jimothy

I made this system not as an attempt to make money but as a project of passion. I love roleplaying games, I love JRPGs, I love isekai, anime, movies, and so much more, and I could just never find a game system that felt right. So I took it upon myself to make one.

I hope that I've made a fun and easy to use system, one that contains complexities for those who enjoy them, mixed with ease of use. At least that is my hope.

I took inspiration from many, many things. It would probably take half the book just explaining them all, but I want it stated that I have no intention of claiming ownership of anything. I borrowed and compiled, and hopefully I have made a system that gives Storytellers and Players the freedom to build a story together and to have fun with it.

I wanted a system that I felt could inhabit any manner of game. My intention with this book is to establish the world I wish to run my games within. But anyone could use this system and place it in their own world, and hopefully it makes perfect sense. You could run a game in the distant past before technology came along, or in the distant future with space-traveling mercenaries. It is my own personal D10 System for all.

Jimothy

Introduction

Welcome to Terrana

Jimothy's D10 Isekai JRPG is a tabletop roleplaying game played with ten-sided dice. One player is the Storyteller, who describes the world, plays everyone the characters meet, and keeps the story moving. Everyone else plays a character of their own making. Together you decide what your characters attempt, and the dice decide how well it goes.

A Note on the World

The Slime King arrived in Terrana about 150 years ago. In that time the world has advanced in technology, science, and magic until it holds many of the comforts of the 20th and 21st centuries of our own world. It is a mismatch of all kinds, adapted around one key difference: where our world struggles with fuel and power, Terrana uses magic in their place.

Terrana has come a long way from the world it once was.

How to Use This Book

The book is organized so you can read what you need, when you need it.

Learning the World

Chapters 1 through 5 cover Terrana itself: where it came from (Chapter 1), its lands and the settings you can play in (Chapter 2), the island nation of Monsera (Chapter 3), the Grand Dungeon (Chapter 4), and the Legends of Terrana, the names at the top of the world's rankings (Chapter 5).

Many lore sections are written by Liris Kant of Monsera University, a scholar who has spent a career studying the peoples and places of Terrana. Read them for flavor and for the things your character would already know. Rules sections are written plainly and are marked by their headings.

Making a Character

Chapter 6 walks through character creation one step at a time, in order. Chapters 7 through 11 hold the options for each step:

Chapter 7: Tropes, Virtues, and Vices

Chapter 8: Heritages

Chapter 9: Merits and Flaws

Chapter 10: Backgrounds

Chapter 11: Willpower, Fate, Sanity, and Mana

Keep a character sheet beside you and fill it in as you go. For a first session, a player only needs Chapter 6 and the chapters it points to. Everything else can be read as it comes up at the table.

Playing the Game

Chapters 12 and 13 cover the rules used during play: Martial Combat Styles (Chapter 12) and Magic (Chapter 13), including the seven casting schools, Battle Aura, and resurrection. They are written for quick lookup in the middle of a session. Chapter 14 covers Equipment and Items, with loot tables, Chapter 15 is a Bestiary of creatures, monster peoples, and bosses with stat blocks, and Chapter 16 covers Alternate Eras for games set in Terrana's past or future. Key terms and conditions are gathered in the index at the end of the book, followed by the battle maps and the combat playmat (each in a light and a dark version), the Summoner's Sheet, and the character sheet.

Giving Feedback

This system is still growing, and play is how it improves. When a rule is unclear, feels off, or does not cover something that happened at your table, flag it. Note the chapter and section, what came up, and how your table handled it in the moment, then pass it along to Jimothy. Specific notes like these shape the next version of the book.
