Introduction

Terrana is full of things that want to eat you, test you, recruit you, or sell you something. This chapter gives every creature in the book, and many more, a stat block a Storyteller can run straight from the page. Most entries are short on purpose: a rank, a few numbers, and the two or three things the creature does best.

Many of the creatures here are people, not monsters. The Bestial Demonfolk, the monster peoples of Monsera, the Immortal Demon Kings, and the bosses of the Grand Dungeon all have homes, jobs, and opinions. A stat block only says how they fight, not whether they should be fought.

How Creatures Work

Every creature is built from its rank. Ranks run from F to SSS. At SS and SSS, power jumps: these are threats that only the greatest heroes, or whole armies, can face.

Attack and Defense: The creature's dice pool for attacking and for defending. It uses the same pool for any defense, and for casting its techniques.

Damage: Its damage pool, rolled like a weapon's. Add the attack's excess successes and compare the total to the target's thresholds.

Health: How many Bashing, Lethal, and Aggravated boxes it has. It takes wound penalties and becomes Incapacitated exactly like a character.

Mana and M/T: Its mana pool and Max Mana Per Turn. Casting a spell costs its normal mana. Mana recovers at the rates in Chapter 11.

Size: Large creatures fill a 2x2 area and add 1 to BDT and 2 boxes to every health column. Huge creatures fill 3x3 and add 2 and 4. Gargantuan creatures fill 4x4 and add 3 and 6. Colossal creatures, such as a Giant Void Kaiju, fill 5x5 and add 4 and 8. These are already included in each entry.

Move: Zones per turn. Flying creatures move at double the number listed for walking; entries that fly list the doubled number.

Elements: A creature's elements decide its Elemental Advantages and which spells it can cast. Elemental Advantages apply to creatures exactly as they do to spells and conjurations (Chapter 13).

Techniques: When an entry says it casts a spell, it uses that spell's full rules from Chapter 13 with the creature's Attack pool.

SumMonz: Any beast or wild fiend of rank S or lower can be caught as a SumMonz (Chapter 13). SS and SSS creatures cannot.

Loot: Use the creature's rank as its Loot Rank (Chapter 14). SS and SSS creatures drop S rank loot and something unique.

Templates

Templates change a creature to fit the story. Stack as many as make sense.

Elite: +1 rank. Use the next rank's numbers.

Boss: Double every health column, two attacks a turn, and immunity to one condition of the Storyteller's choice. Dungeon bosses already include it.

Swarm: Many small bodies as one creature filling a 2x2 area. Area attacks deal +2 damage to it, and it cannot be Rooted or knocked Prone.

Pack Alpha: Every pack member in sight adds 1 die to attacks. If the alpha falls, the pack must roll Composure or flee.

Elemental: Add one element. The creature gains a rank 2 spell of that school and takes half damage from its own element.

Undead: Immune to Poisoned and Bleeding. Takes +2 damage from Bright Magic and Bright-imbued weapons.

Voidtouched: Everyone who sees it for the first time makes a Sanity roll, and each of its hits drains 1 or more mana.

Dungeon Employee: Respawns in its quarters when defeated. Fights hard, but fights fair, and will not hit a delver who has yielded.
