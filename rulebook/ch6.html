Introduction

Building a character is a series of meaningful choices that together define who your character is, what they are good at, what they struggle with, and what keeps them going when things get difficult. The choices interact in ways that reward thought without requiring optimization. Start with the character that interests you, and everything else follows from that.

The Creation Process

Character creation follows these thirteen steps in order. Each step points to the chapter that covers it in full.

Step 1. Pick your Trope: Choose one Trope, the personality archetype at the heart of your character. Every Trope has a Boon and a Bane. The Boon gives you one bonus die on rolls with an ability tied to the Trope. The Bane gives you one penalty die on rolls with a fixed ability. Record your Trope at the top of your sheet. The full list is in Chapter 7.

Step 2. Consult the Scales: Your sheet has seven Virtue and Vice scales: Humility and Pride, Charity and Greed, Chastity and Lust, Patience and Wrath, Temperance and Gluttony, Kindness and Envy, and Diligence and Sloth. Mark where your character sits on each one:

Two highs: two scales at High Virtue or High Vice, the 2 at either end.

Two lows: two scales at Low Virtue or Low Vice, the 1 on either side.

Three central: the other three scales stay at Central, the 0 in the middle.

Then choose two Driving Factors, one for each of your highs. A Driving Factor is what your character wants, and it must match the scale and the side its high sits on. Your highs also set your starting Willpower in Step 9. The scales and every Driving Factor are covered in Chapter 7, Part 2.

Step 3. Assign your Role: Your Role is your position on the battlefield: a line (Frontline, Midline, or Backline) followed by one word for how you fight, such as Frontline Tank, Frontline Fighter, Midline Healer, Midline Support, Backline Mage, or Backline Rogue. Your Role decides where you start when combat begins. You can change it as play goes on if you want to try a different line of attack.

Step 4. Choose your Heritage: Pick your heritage from Chapter 8 and record what it gives you: your Innate Attribute (it starts at 2 dots; note Innate beside it), your Innate Flaw, your Attribute Flaw, your Heritage Merit, and your free Heritage magic dot. Rare heritages need Storyteller approval.

Step 5. Allocate your Attributes: Every attribute starts at 1 dot. Give each column (Physical, Social, and Mental) one of three designations, and write its number in the space next to the column's name on your sheet:

Affinity: write 6.

Standard: write 5.

Aversion: write 4.

That number is the column's cap, and it is also how many dots you spend across that column's three attributes. Your Attribute Flaw column can never be your Affinity. No attribute can be filled above 5 at creation. A Hyuman with Hyuman Adaptability writes 5 next to all three columns instead. Your Physical and Mental designations also set your dots in Steps 10 and 11.

Step 6. Prioritize your Abilities: Abilities start at 0 dots. Give each of the six ability groups a different number from 2 to 7 and write it next to the group's name on your sheet. That number is how many dots you spend in that group, and no ability can be filled above 5 at creation.

Any ability with 4 or more dots gets one free specialty: write a condition beside it, such as Awareness: Scent, for a bonus die when a roll fits. Then choose your Past Life Occupation, what your character did before arriving in Terrana, and write PL next to the abilities tied to it.

Step 7. Select your Merits and Flaws: You have 7 points to spend on Merits. Each Flaw point you take gives you 1 more point, up to 7 more, for a maximum of 14. Record them in the Merits, Flaws, and Backgrounds section, along with your Heritage Merit. The full lists are in Chapter 9.

Step 8. Determine your Backgrounds: You have 7 points to spend on Backgrounds, plus any points left over from Step 7. Record them in the same section. Backgrounds are listed in Chapter 10.

Step 9. Set Willpower, Fate, and Sanity: Fill in 5 dots of each. If both of your highs are Vices, fill in only 4 dots of Willpower. If both are Virtues, fill in 6. One of each leaves it at 5. Fate and Sanity can only be changed at creation by Merits and Flaws (Chapter 9). All three are covered in full in Chapter 11.

Step 10. Choose your Combat Styles: Your Physical column decides how many dots you spend on Combat Styles: 7 for Affinity, 5 for Standard, or 3 for Aversion. Spread them across any styles you like, up to 5 dots in one style, and mark each style with a P for Primary. Each dot unlocks that rank's Default Ability, plus your choice of its Option A or Option B (Chapter 12).

Step 11. Choose your Magical Knowledges: Your Mental column decides how many dots you spend on Magical Knowledges (casting schools, circle magic, and Battle Aura): 7 for Affinity, 5 for Standard, or 3 for Aversion, up to 5 dots in one knowledge. Mark each with a P for Primary. Your free Heritage magic dot from Step 4 is written here too, also marked with a P, and does not use these dots. For a casting school, each dot lets you choose one of that rank's two spells (Chapter 13).

Step 12. Spend your Freebie Points: You have 7 Freebie Points, though the Storyteller may adjust this. Mana starts at 1 dot, and this is the only time it can be raised during creation. Each dot costs:

Attribute: 3 points.

Ability: 2 points.

Combat Style or Magical Knowledge: 4 points. A new style or knowledge bought here is marked with an S for Secondary.

Background: 1 point.

Mana: 7 points.

Freebies cannot be spent on Merits, Flaws, Willpower, Fate, Sanity, or Heritage traits, and the Golden Rule still applies.

Step 13. Calculate your Level: Add up all your dots in Attributes (including Mana), Abilities, Combat Styles, Magical Knowledges (including Heritage magic), and Merits, Flaws, and Backgrounds. Write the total in the Level box. Willpower, Fate, Sanity, and Virtues and Vices do not count.

The Golden Rule

At character creation, nothing on your sheet may be filled in above 5 dots, not even with Freebie Points, no matter what modifier, bonus, or heritage benefit applies. There are no exceptions, including your Innate Attribute.

The cap is the highest any attribute in that column can ever go, while the Golden Rule only limits what you fill in during creation. Your Innate Attribute's maximum is 1 higher than its column's cap.

P and S Markers: Only styles and knowledges taken in Steps 10 and 11, plus your free Heritage magic dot, get a P for Primary. Anything gained later, even with Freebie Points, gets an S for Secondary. P costs new dot x5 XP to raise; S costs x6.

Damage Thresholds

Your thresholds decide what kind of damage an incoming attack or damage source does.

Bashing Damage Threshold (BDT): Fortitude + Armor.

Lethal Damage Threshold (LDT): BDT x2.

Aggravated Damage Threshold (ADT): BDT x3.

Specialties

A specialty is a condition, written next to an ability, that gives you a bonus die when a roll fits it (for example, Awareness: Scent). Each ability can have up to 3 specialties. Any ability with 4 or more dots at character creation gets one free specialty. Every other specialty costs 5 XP and needs Storyteller approval.

Past Life Occupation

Choose your character's Past Life Occupation: what they did, or loved doing, before they arrived in Terrana. It does not have to match the player's own life. Write PL next to each ability tied to that profession or hobby. Raising a PL ability costs the new dot x2 in XP, one less than a normal ability. That is the only benefit.

Mana

Everything in Terrana has some access to mana. Mana is rated from 1 to 10 dots, the only trait with ten. Every character starts with 1 dot. At creation, Mana can only be raised with Freebie Points, at 7 points per dot. After creation, each dot costs the new dot x7 in XP.

Mana Pool: (Magic dots + Circle Magic dots) x Mana. Some items in the world let you add other abilities, such as Alchemy, Attunement, or Implements, but at creation and in most cases the pool uses only those two abilities.

Maximum Mana Per Turn: Fortitude + Mana. This is the most mana you can safely spend in a single turn, no matter how large your pool is.

How mana recovers, and everything else about Mana, Willpower, Fate, and Sanity, is covered in Chapter 11.

Earning and Spending XP

Earning XP

At the end of each chapter of the story, every player earns XP:

1 XP for free, just for playing.

1 XP for acting: playing your character in the scene.

1 XP for answering a question the Storyteller asks, or for sharing something you learned that chapter.

1 bonus XP from your Affinity column, if you earned it (see below).

Physical Affinity: Earn the bonus if your character took part in combat or made a big physical gesture during the chapter.

Mental Affinity: Earn the bonus if your character used magic, or made a profound discovery or had a profound idea, during the chapter.

Social Affinity: Earn the bonus automatically, as long as your character took part in the chapter in any way. If everyone at the table earned a bonus XP that chapter, you earn one more, for a total of 2 bonus XP.

Spending XP and Freebie Points

Starting characters receive 7 Freebie Points, which the Storyteller may adjust. XP costs are always the new dot's value times the listed number.
