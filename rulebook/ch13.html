Introduction

Magic in Terrana is not a gift handed to a chosen few. It is a force that moves through the world and through every living thing in it. Mana cycles through the soil, the sky, and the blood. Learning to shape it is less like discovering a talent and more like learning to breathe deliberately. Anyone willing to put in the work can reach it. Very few reach the higher ranks.

This chapter covers four kinds of magic. Battle Aura pours mana through the body and weapon and pairs with the Martial Combat Styles of Chapter 12. Part One covers the seven casting schools: the combat-legal disciplines any character can learn through training, study, and hard-earned experience. Part Two covers combination magic: spells that emerge when two or more schools are cast together as one effect, producing things neither school could achieve alone. Part Three covers circle magic: ritual-based magic that works outside the seven schools and outside the combat turn structure entirely.

Every spell entry in this chapter follows the same format. Mana cost. Speed. Type. Range. Dice pool. Effect. The description is flavor. The effect block is mechanical law. If an entry does not answer your question, flag it specifically so the rule can be refined through future play.

Mana itself (your Mana Pool, Max Mana Per Turn, recovery, Mana Burn, attuned and committed mana, and running dry) is covered in Chapter 11.

How Casting Works

Dice Pools

When casting a spell, the basic dice pool is Attribute plus Magical Ability plus dots in the relevant school, up to the spell's rank. The spell entry states which Attribute and Ability apply. Mana spent to enhance a roll adds 1 die per mana, declared before rolling, assigned to either the attack roll or the damage roll. Not both at once. The amount of mana that can enhance a roll is capped at your dots in that school or your M/T, whichever is lower.

What Every Spell Entry Tells You

Mana Cost: Total mana to cast

Speed: Instant, Reaction, or Extended

Type: One or more keywords describing the spell's function

Range: Target zone, self, or AoE shape

Dice Pool: Exact roll to make

Effect: The mechanical outcome

Description: Flavor text

The defensive roll is always stated in the Effect. If a spell entry does not name what the target rolls to resist, flag it specifically so the entry can be corrected.

Defenders Choose Their Defense

A target being attacked by magic chooses their own defense unless the spell explicitly says otherwise. Each spell entry lists the default defense, which is the roll that makes the most sense for what the spell physically does:

Dexterity + Dodge: getting out of the way of bolts, projectiles, spikes, grasping vines, and other targeted attacks

Strength + Athletics: bracing against being pushed, pulled, or dragged, and breaking free once held

Dexterity + Acrobatics: keeping your footing on slick or shifting ground and landing safely from a fall

Fortitude + Endurance: resisting effects that work on the body, such as cold, poison, exhaustion, burning terrain, and mana drain

A defender can also try a situational defense when it fits, such as catching a large thrown object or bracing to tank it. Counter-magic follows the Magical Resistance rules below.

Upkeep and Persistent Spells

A Persistent spell has a duration counted in turns. Each Upkeep Phase the countdown ticks down by 1. Paying the upkeep cost during the Upkeep Phase freezes the countdown for that turn. The upkeep cost for any spell is the spell's mana cost minus 1.

Countdown spells are a subtype of Persistent. Duration equals half the total mana spent, rounded up. Paying upkeep (cost minus 1) during the Upkeep Phase freezes the countdown for one turn. The countdown resumes on any turn upkeep is not paid.

Magical Resistance

When a spell targets a character who wants to resist its effect, the defender rolls a resistance pool that includes their dots in the relevant magic. Most resistance rolls are written as Fortitude plus Endurance with the caster's school dots added. Resistance is a separate roll, not a comparison against the attacker's successes. The spell entry names the defensive roll.

Upcasting

Additional mana beyond a spell's base cost can be spent to either add dice to the roll or augment the spell's effect. Both options cannot be chosen at once. Declare before rolling. For combination spells, augmentation costs extra mana scaled to the number of schools involved: 2-school spells cost 2 mana per augmentation, 3-school cost 3, and so on.

Mana Bleed: Any effect that makes a target lose mana during an Upkeep Phase is Mana Bleed. Most Mana Bleed comes from Shade Magic and the combination spells built on it. Losing mana all at once from a single hit is a drain, not a bleed.

Prepared Spells

A caster does not have open access to every spell they know during combat. Spells must be prepared ahead of time. Preparation happens outside of combat and represents the caster arranging their intent, attunement, and focus before the fight.

The number of spells a character can have prepared at any time is equal to their dots in Intelligence. A character with 3 Intelligence can have 3 spells prepared. A character with 5 can have 5.

Prepared spells can be swapped freely outside of combat. There is no cost and no time restriction beyond being out of a fight. In combat, the prepared list is fixed until the fight ends.

A spell from any school the character knows can fill a prepared slot, including combination spells the character meets the prerequisites for. The prepared list does not need to be filled entirely. A character may go into a fight with fewer prepared spells than their Intelligence allows.

Learning the Casting Schools

Every dot in a casting school also grants a default ability. It comes with the dot automatically, in addition to the spell option chosen for that dot. Default abilities come in three kinds. Some cost mana each time they are used. Some are attuned: they hold part of your M/T for as long as they are active, and ending one returns that mana to your M/T during the following Upkeep Phase. Some are permanent improvements to how that school's spells work.

The seven casting schools are the combat-legal magical disciplines available to any character willing to train in them. Each is a standalone knowledge tracked under Magical Knowledges on the sheet. Each runs from Core (dot 1) through Cosmic (dot 7), with two technique options available at each dot. When a dot is purchased the player chooses one option. The unchosen option can be bought later for Rank x2 XP.

A school designated as Primary costs new dot times 5 XP. Any other school costs new dot times 6 XP. Every school taken in Step 11 of character creation is Primary and marked with a P; a school gained any other way is Secondary and marked with an S.

The Rank scale for Magical Knowledges is: Core (1) / Terrestrial (2) / Aerial (3) / Lunar (4) / Stellar (5) / Galactic (6) / Cosmic (7).

Battle Aura

Battle Aura is not one of the seven casting schools. It is a separate discipline of physical enhancement that runs mana through the body and weapon. It can be used alongside any of the seven schools but is learned and tracked independently under Magical Knowledges.

Battle Aura does not produce elemental effects on its own. From Rank 4 it can carry the energy of one casting school through imbuing (see Imbuing below).

Mana assigned to Battle Aura can go to either the attack roll or the damage roll. Declare before rolling.

Starting at Rank 4, techniques may be activated by spending Willpower instead of their mana cost. Rank 4 costs 1 Willpower, Rank 5 costs 2 Willpower, Rank 6 costs 3 Willpower, and Rank 7 costs 4 Willpower. Additional mana can still be spent on top of a Willpower activation for further enhancement. Spending Willpower this way is not an action and does not cost AP. It is separate from the 1 Willpower you may spend each turn to push or reroll (Chapter 11), and each point paid marks an X on your Willpower row.

Default Abilities

Like the seven casting schools, every Battle Aura dot grants a default ability. It comes with the dot automatically, in addition to the technique chosen for that dot. As with the casting schools, the unchosen technique can be bought later for Rank x2 XP.

Battle Aura and Combat Styles

Battle Aura is built to work with every Combat Style. Wherever a Battle Aura dice pool lists Attribute + Ability + Combat Style, use the Attribute and Ability of the Combat Style you are fighting with, plus your dots in that Style.

Armor Penetration ignores points of the armor portion of a target’s DT. Magic Penetration ignores points of the portion added by spells, enchantments, or magical gear. Penetration from different sources adds together, but neither can lower its portion below 0. Several Combat Styles grant Armor Penetration of their own, and Aura Edge (Rank 2) adds both kinds to every weapon and unarmed attack. The Martial Combat Styles chapter has the full rules.

Imbuing (Rank 4)

Starting at Rank 4, a practitioner can imbue an attack with the energy of one of the seven casting schools: Fire, Water, Air, Earth, Nature, Bright, or Shade. This is not combination magic. Combination magic weaves casting schools together into a new spell. Imbuing simply carries a school's energy on a Battle Aura technique or a weapon or unarmed attack.

An imbued attack takes on the nature of that school. A Fire-imbued strike is a fire attack, a Water-imbued strike is a water attack, and so on, including Elemental Advantages and that school's mana assignment rule (see Imbuing Attacks). The practitioner may spend additional mana on the imbued attack up to their Battle Aura dots plus their dots in the imbued school, or their M/T, whichever comes first. An attack can be imbued with only one school at a time, and the practitioner needs at least 1 dot in the school they imbue.

Flight (Rank 6)

Empowered Flight is unlocked at Rank 6. Taking off costs 6 mana, or 3 Willpower. Moving at altitude costs an upkeep of 5 mana each Upkeep Phase, and hovering in place costs half that, rounded up (3 mana). While flying you move at double your normal movement. Taking damage that meets your BDT while airborne requires a Dexterity + Athletics roll against the damage dealt. On a failure you begin to fall, land during your next Upkeep Phase, and take falling damage as normal.

Winged flyers use the Athletics specialty Flight instead and need no mana. Anything that flies moves at double its normal movement. Mechanical flight (machines, vehicles, and devices) must use all of its movement every turn or begin to fall.

The practitioner can feel and deliberately direct their fighting spirit for the first time. The enhancement is small but real.

Combination magic is what happens when two or more casting schools are woven together as a unified effect. The result is a distinct spell neither school could produce alone. Every combination spell is learned separately. Meeting the prerequisites does not automatically grant access to the spell.

Healing Magic

No single casting school can heal. Healing comes only from combination magic. Bright Magic on its own does not heal anyone.

A healing spell does not repair damage directly. Radiant energy surrounds the target as they are given a PHP (Pool of Healing Points) equal to the cast successes. The healing does not begin on its own. The target spends points from the pool during their own turn. Spending PHP does not take an action.

Healing Tiers

Every healing spell names its healing tier. The tier decides what each point in the pool can do. Each point spent buys one option from that tier. To slake a damage box is to lower it one type (Aggravated to Lethal, or Lethal to Bashing). To neutralize a box is to remove it.

Basic: neutralize 1 Bashing, or slake 1 Lethal to Bashing. Cannot affect Aggravated.

Advanced: neutralize 2 Bashing, neutralize 1 Lethal, or slake 1 Aggravated to Lethal.

Expert: neutralize 4 Bashing, neutralize 2 Lethal, or neutralize 1 Aggravated.

Master healing regrows lost limbs and is never used in combat. Resurrection is the only tier above Master (see Resurrection).

How Long a Pool Lasts

Every healing spell states how long its pool lasts. Many pools last until the end of the target's next turn, and any unused points are lost. Stronger spells let the energy linger using the countdown mechanic: the pool lasts a number of turns equal to half the mana spent, rounded up, and paying the spell's upkeep (mana cost minus 1) during the Upkeep Phase freezes the countdown for that turn. When the pool ends, any unused points are lost.

Resurrection

Death in Terrana is not always the end, but coming back is rare.

The Countdown: When a character dies without choosing to, the mana they held at the moment of death stays in the body and counts down by 1 each day. While any remains, the soul can still be called back.

Rising Undead: If the count reaches 0 and the body was not cremated, it soaks up the ambient mana around it and rises as mindless undead. Sentient undead are far rarer: they take an act of a god, or a personality implanted by a powerful Shade user.

A Chosen Death: A character who chose Blaze of Glory chose their death and cannot be resurrected.

The Spell: Only one spell can call someone back: Resurrection, a six-school combination of Fire, Water, Air, Nature, Bright, and Shade at 6 dots each (Six-School Combinations). Very few people alive have the training to cast it, which is why resurrection is precious and never routine. Healing magic can repair a body, but only Resurrection can bring back the person in it.

Imbuing Attacks

A weapon or unarmed attack can carry the energy of a casting school in two ways: Battle Aura imbuing, available from Battle Aura Rank 4, and Augmentation, a circle magic that builds magic into weapons with an augment slot. Either way, an attack can carry only one school's energy at a time.

When an attack carries a school's energy, that school's fixed rule decides which roll its mana can go to:

Fire: Damage roll only

Water: Either roll

Air: Attack roll only

Earth: Damage roll only

Nature: Either roll

Bright: Attack roll only

Shade: Damage roll only

Battle Aura: Either roll

Imbuing is not a spell. It does not produce a spell block with type, range, or defensive roll. It is mana fed into an existing physical attack.

Elemental Advantages

The five combat magic schools do not exist in isolation. They interact with one another in structured ways, and understanding those interactions is one of the most important things a caster can learn. Some elements carry inherent advantages against others, making attacks more effective, walls harder to break, and spell effects more difficult to resist. Ignoring this will cost you. Using it well can turn a fight before your opponent realizes what is happening.

Every element has a strong advantage against one other element and a moderate advantage against a second. The reverse is also true: every element is weakened against two others. These relationships are fixed and apply any time two elements interact in combat, whether through direct spell attacks, elemental walls and shields, terrain effects, or combination spells that carry elemental tags. An advantage needs something elemental on the other side to oppose: a wall, shield, terrain, or elemental effect, or a creature with an elemental type. A summoned water elemental, for example, is struck by Air Magic with advantage and by Fire Magic with disadvantage.

How Advantages Work

A strong advantage (+2) means the attacking element gains 2 additional dice on rolls made against the target element. This applies to attack rolls when the spell directly opposes an elemental effect, and to the damage roll when striking a target who is protected by or empowered with the opposing element. When attacking an elemental wall or shield, the defending element's DT bonus is also affected: the shielded target gains double the bonus against an element it is strong against, and half the bonus against an element it is weak against.

A moderate advantage (+1) works the same way but adds only 1 die. It is still meaningful, particularly at higher ranks where the difference of a single die can push a result past a Damage Threshold.

These bonuses apply in both directions. A Fire mage attacking a Water wall is rolling at a disadvantage. A Water mage attacking a Fire wall is rolling with 2 extra dice. Players and Storytellers should keep this in mind when choosing which spell to cast into which situation.

Reading the diagram: each element's strong advantage points to the element immediately clockwise on the outer ring. Each element's moderate advantage points to the element two steps clockwise via the inner star line.

Quick Reference

Fire: +2 against Nature, +1 against Earth

Nature: +2 against Earth, +1 against Air

Earth: +2 against Air, +1 against Water

Air: +2 against Water, +1 against Fire

Water: +2 against Fire, +1 against Nature

Bright Magic and Shade Magic have no elemental strengths or weaknesses. They do not interact with the elemental advantage system. Their advantages and disadvantages are tied entirely to living and undead targets rather than to opposing elemental forces.

Combination spells carry the elemental tags of every school used to cast them. A combination spell using Fire and Water, for example, is both strong and weak depending on what it encounters. The Storyteller and player should note which schools are involved in a combination cast and apply the relevant bonuses and penalties accordingly.

Part One: The Casting Magics

The seven casting schools are Fire, Water, Air, Earth, Nature, Bright, and Shade. Each runs from Core (1) to Cosmic (7). Every rank grants a Default Ability and offers two spells: choose one when you buy the dot.

Fire Magic

Fire Magic is the school of combustion, heat, and destruction by burning. Its spells deal damage directly and impose the Burning condition. Fire is aggressive and direct. It gains mana assignment to the damage roll only. It carries elemental advantages against Nature and Earth and is weakest against Water and Air.

Fire Magic has elemental interactions with elemental defenses and conjurations: +2 against Nature, +1 against Earth, -1 against Air, -2 against Water.

Rank 1 Core

The practitioner can kindle and hurl small flames. Fire at this rank is close-range and straightforward. There is nothing subtle about it.

Default Ability: Heat Tolerance (Permanent) Damage you take from ordinary, non-magical heat and flame is halved (rounded down), and you can briefly handle hot coals or metal.

Ignite

Mana Cost: 1

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Fire

Range: Adjacent zone

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Launch a small flame at a target in an adjacent zone. On a hit the target takes damage equal to excess successes. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge. Outside of combat, Ignite can set an unattended combustible object on fire, and that fire burns as ordinary flame.

A flick of concentrated heat thrown directly at a target.

Kindle

Mana Cost: 1

Speed: Instant

Type: Utility, Fire

Range: Self / touch

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Set fire to any flammable material the caster touches or holds. Alternatively, light a small flame on something that can burn, such as a torch or candle. Once lit it is ordinary fire and burns as normal. Cannot be used as an attack. Can destroy documents, ignite traps, or light a campfire with one success.

The most basic application of fire. Make a thing burn.

Rank 2 Terrestrial

The caster can project fire to greater distances and begin to shape it into persistent effects.

Default Ability: Warmth (Attuned: holds 1 of your M/T while active) You and allies in adjacent zones are unaffected by non-magical cold and cannot gain the Chilled condition from weather.

Fire Bolt

Mana Cost: 2

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Fire

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A bolt of fire strikes a single target at any range within line of sight. On a hit deals damage equal to excess successes and applies the Burning condition. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge or appropriate cover.

A reliable aimed bolt. Goes where it is aimed. Burns what it hits.

Burning Ground

Mana Cost: 2

Speed: Instant

Type: Utility, Persistent, Fire

Range: Zone

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Cover a zone in low flames. Any target entering or remaining in the zone must roll Fortitude + Endurance vs cast successes each Upkeep Phase. Failure deals 1 damage and applies the Burning condition. Water Magic applied to the zone reduces cast successes by the Water caster's successes. Upkeep: 1.

The ground burns. Not intensely. But it burns.

Rank 3 Aerial

Fire becomes a cone and a shield. The caster can now catch themselves with it.

Default Ability: Reach of Flame (Permanent) Your Fire spells with a range of Adjacent zone can target a zone one step further away.

Fire Breath

Mana Cost: 3

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, AoE, Fire

Range: AoE-Cone

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A cone of flame erupts from the caster, covering all zones directly in front and diagonally forward. Caster rolls once and applies the same attack successes to all targets. Each target defends separately. All targets hit gain the Burning condition. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

A broad expulsion of fire from the caster's hands or mouth.

Flame Wall

Mana Cost: 3

Speed: Instant

Type: Conjuration, Persistent, Fire

Range: Adjacent zone border

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A wall of flame rises along a border between two zones. Any target attempting to cross the border must roll Fortitude + Endurance vs cast successes or take damage equal to excess successes and gain the Burning condition. The wall provides cover to targets on either side. Water Magic reduces cast successes by the Water caster's successes. Upkeep: 2.

A vertical sheet of fire that punishes anyone who walks through it.

Rank 4 Lunar

The caster can wrap themselves in fire as a persistent defense and project fire in straight lines.

Default Ability: Flare (2 mana, Reaction) When an enemy in an adjacent zone attacks you, a flash of fire bursts from your body. That attack loses 2 dice.

Mana Cost: 4

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Fire

Range: Ranged, straight line through any number of zones

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A whip of concentrated flame projects in a straight line from the caster, hitting every target in that line. Caster rolls once and applies the same attack successes to all targets. Each target defends separately. All targets hit gain the Burning condition. Damage is rolled separately after a successful hit.

A lash of fire that cuts a straight line through everything in its path.

Ember Cloak

Mana Cost: 4

Speed: Instant, Extended

Type: Defense, Persistent, Fire

Range: Self

Dice Pool: Fortitude + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: The caster wraps themselves in a sustained layer of fire. Any target who strikes the caster in melee takes damage equal to half the original cast successes (rounded down) and gains the Burning condition. The cloak also adds the cast successes to the caster's BDT against water-based attacks. Upkeep: 3.

The caster burns. Anyone who reaches for them learns it immediately.

Rank 5 Stellar

Fire can be launched as a large explosion or concentrated into armor-piercing intensity.

Default Ability: Searing (Permanent) Damage from your Fire spells ignores 1 point of the armor portion of a target's DT.

Fireball

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, AoE, Fire

Range: AoE-4 (2x2 zone area), any targeted zone within line of sight

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A sphere of fire detonates in a 2x2 zone area. Caster rolls once and applies the same attack successes to all targets in the area. Each target defends separately. All targets hit gain the Burning condition. Can hit allies. The caster may choose any adjacent 2x2 zone area within line of sight.

The classic. A ball of fire that explodes on impact and covers the area.

Piercing Flame

Mana Cost: 5

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Armor Pen, Fire

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: A focused lance of fire heated beyond normal flame burns through defenses. Carries Armor Pen: before rolling, declare additional mana toward Armor Pen. Every 2 mana reduces the armor portion of the target's DT by 1 for this attack. Target hit gains the Burning condition. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge or appropriate cover.

Fire focused down to a point of intense penetration.

Rank 6 Galactic

The caster can rain fire from above and animate a sustained fire entity.

Default Ability: Living Flame (Attuned: holds 2 of your M/T while active) Your body is wreathed in fire. Any target that hits you with a melee attack gains the Burning condition.

Rain of Fire

Mana Cost: 6

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, AoE, Fire

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Fire falls from above across a number of zones up to the caster's Fire dots. The caster rolls once and applies the same attack successes to all targets in all chosen zones. Each target defends separately. All targets hit gain the Burning condition. The caster must be able to see or have previously visited every chosen zone. Water Magic in a targeted zone reduces cast successes in that zone by the Water caster's successes.

The sky over multiple zones ignites. Everyone below learns at once.

Mana Cost: 6

Speed: Instant

Range: Any adjacent zone

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Summons a fire elemental in an adjacent zone. It cannot act on the turn it is summoned. On subsequent turns the caster may command it to attack one target in its zone or an adjacent zone, using the caster's Fire dice pool. Any target it hits gains the Burning condition. The elemental has a Vitality pool equal to double the cast successes. Water Magic reduces the Vitality pool by the Water caster's successes. Upkeep: 5.

A semi-autonomous entity of concentrated flame that attacks on command.

Rank 7 Cosmic

Fire at this rank is an extinction event for a zone or a conflagration that envelops every target on the field.

Default Ability: Heart of the Sun (Permanent) Burning applied by your Fire spells deals 1 Lethal on a successful resistance roll and 1 Aggravated on a failed one.

Inferno

Mana Cost: 7

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, AoE, Fire

Range: All zones within line of sight

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: Fire envelops every zone the caster can see. Caster rolls once. Every target in every visible zone must defend. Each target defends separately. All targets hit gain the Burning condition. The caster's own zone is affected. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

The world, briefly, is on fire.

Eternal Flame

Mana Cost: 7

Speed: Instant

Type: Utility, Persistent, Fire

Range: Zone

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Fire dots

Effect: The caster creates a fire that will not die naturally. It fills the zone permanently, dealing damage equal to cast successes to any target who enters or remains, and applying the Burning condition. It cannot be extinguished by normal means. Water Magic reduces cast successes by the Water caster's successes but cannot dispel it without matching or exceeding the successes entirely. If the successes are fully matched the Eternal Flame is extinguished. Upkeep: 6.

A fire that stays. It does not go out. It does not diminish. It simply burns.

Water Magic

Water Magic is the school of fluid force, cold, and flow. Its spells deal damage, apply the Chilled condition, move targets, and can shift between offense and defense with notable flexibility. Water can assign mana to either the attack roll or the damage roll. It carries elemental advantages against Fire and Nature and is weakest against Air and Earth.

Elemental interactions: +2 against Fire, +1 against Nature, -1 against Earth, -2 against Air.

Rank 1 Core

Water at this rank is a jet and a chill. The caster can hurl water with force and apply cold to surfaces or targets.

Default Ability: Water Breathing (Attuned: holds 1 of your M/T while active) You can breathe underwater and swim at your normal movement.

Water Bolt

Mana Cost: 1

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Water

Range: Adjacent zone

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Water dots

Effect: A high-pressure bolt of water hits a target in an adjacent zone. On a hit deals damage equal to excess successes. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

Water moving very fast in one direction. It hits hard.

Chill Touch

Mana Cost: 1

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Utility, Water

Range: Touch

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: The caster applies intense cold through touch. Against an unwilling target this is a touch attack: on a hit the target takes damage equal to excess successes. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge. Outside of combat, Chill Touch can freeze a small amount of water, frost a surface, or chill food and drink, and the cold fades naturally.

Cold applied directly. Not violence yet. Just cold.

Rank 2 Terrestrial

Water can be projected at range and used to create treacherous terrain.

Default Ability: Purify (1 mana, Instant) Make up to a zone of water clean and safe to drink, or draw a few gallons of fresh water out of the air.

Water Jet

Mana Cost: 2

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, CC, Water

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Water dots

Effect: A focused stream of pressurized water hits a single target within line of sight. On a hit deals damage equal to excess successes. Any target hit must roll Strength + Athletics vs cast successes or be pushed 1 zone away from the caster. Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

A stream of water that pushes as hard as it hits.

Ice Patch

Mana Cost: 2

Speed: Instant

Type: Utility, Persistent, Water

Range: Zone

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: Freezes the surface of a zone with a thin layer of ice. The zone becomes difficult terrain. Any target entering or moving through the zone must roll Dexterity + Acrobatics vs cast successes or fall prone (Knocked Down condition). Fire Magic applied to the zone reduces cast successes by the Fire caster's successes. Upkeep: 1.

The floor gets slick. The transition from upright to down is fast.

Rank 3 Aerial

The caster can reach further with ice and begin to use water as a reactive defense.

Default Ability: Flowing Form (Permanent) You gain 1 bonus die on Dexterity + Dodge rolls against Fire Magic.

Frost Lance

Mana Cost: 3

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, Water

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Water dots

Effect: A lance of ice fires at a target within line of sight. On a hit deals damage equal to excess successes and applies the Chilled condition. On 3+ excess successes the Chilled condition escalates to Frozen (the target cannot move at all and loses their movement action until freed, which requires either Fire Magic applied to them or a Strength + Athletics roll vs cast successes as an action). Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

Cold compressed to a point and thrown. It gets into the target in more ways than one.

Water Shield

Mana Cost: 3

Speed: Reaction

Type: Defense, Water

Range: Self

Dice Pool: Fortitude + Magic + Water dots

Effect: When an attack is declared against the caster, spend 3 mana as a Reaction to raise a barrier of rushing water. Roll Fortitude + Magic + Water dots. Each success adds 1 to the caster's DT against this specific attack. Against fire-based attacks, double the DT bonus from this roll. Against earth or nature-based attacks, the shield provides no bonus.

Water intercedes between the caster and whatever is coming.

Rank 4 Lunar

Water can fully freeze targets and put out fire across a whole zone.

Default Ability: Water Walking (Attuned: holds 2 of your M/T while active) You can move across the surface of water and other liquids as if it were solid ground.

Flash Freeze

Mana Cost: 4

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, CC, Water

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: Water erupts from the ground around a target, instantly freezing them in place. Target must roll Fortitude + Endurance vs cast successes. On failure, the target gains the Frozen condition (cannot move, loses movement action, -2 dice to all rolls while frozen). On 3+ excess successes the target also takes damage equal to excess successes as the ice compresses. Fire Magic applied to the target reduces the successes on the freeze and can end it. Defend: Fortitude + Endurance.

Sudden total freezing. Not cold. Frozen.

Quench

Mana Cost: 4

Speed: Instant

Type: Utility, Water

Range: Zone within line of sight

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: Water floods a zone and puts out fire. Every Burning condition on targets in the zone ends. Each Fire Magic effect in the zone (walls, burning ground, fire conjurations) loses successes equal to the cast successes, and any reduced to 0 ends. On 3+ successes the zone also becomes difficult terrain until the start of the next Upkeep Phase.

Every flame in the zone goes out at once.

Rank 5 Stellar

Water can draw targets toward the caster and form persistent body armor from ice.

Default Ability: Deep Cold (Permanent) A target that gains the Chilled condition from your Water spells also loses their free move while Chilled.

Tidal Pull

Mana Cost: 5

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, CC, Water

Range: Targeted

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Water dots

Effect: A wave of water crashes against a single target and drags them toward the caster. On a hit deals damage equal to excess successes. Any target hit must roll Strength + Athletics vs cast successes or be dragged 1 zone toward the caster per 2 excess successes (minimum 1 zone on failure). Defend: Dexterity + Dodge.

A wave that hits a target and pulls them back with it.

Ice Armor

Mana Cost: 5

Speed: Instant, Extended

Type: Defense, Persistent, Water

Range: Self

Dice Pool: Fortitude + Magic + Water dots

Effect: The caster encases themselves in a layer of magical ice. Add cast successes to the caster's BDT against all physical attacks. Additionally, the ice absorbs damage: the first time the caster would take damage each round, reduce that damage by the cast successes before applying it. Fire attacks deal double damage to the Ice Armor, reducing the BDT bonus by the fire damage dealt. Upkeep: 4.

Ice plate. It holds until it is melted. Which takes work.

Rank 6 Galactic

Water at this rank can blanket multiple zones in ice and redirect incoming attacks.

Default Ability: Slip Away (3 mana, Reaction) When you would be pushed, pulled, or dragged, you flow like water instead. Ignore the forced movement and move 1 zone in a direction of your choice.

Blizzard

Mana Cost: 6

Speed: Instant

Type: Attack, AoE, Water

Range: AoE, up to Water dots zones chosen by caster

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: Ice and freezing wind blast across a number of zones up to the caster's Water dots. Caster rolls once. Every target in every chosen zone must defend. Each defends separately. All targets hit gain the Chilled condition. On 3+ excess successes, a target gains Frozen instead. Fire Magic in a targeted zone reduces cast successes in that zone. Defend: Fortitude + Endurance.

Multiple zones become extremely cold and hostile all at once.

Countercurrent

Speed: Reaction

Type: Defense, Water

Range: Self or adjacent ally

Dice Pool: Dexterity + Magic + Water dots

Effect: When the caster or an adjacent ally is the target of a ranged attack or targeted spell, spend 6 mana as a Reaction. Roll Dexterity + Magic + Water dots. If the roll's successes exceed the attack's successes, the attack is redirected to a target of the caster's choice within range of the original attack. If the successes do not exceed the attack's, the attack proceeds normally. Cannot be used against melee attacks.

An incoming attack gets turned around and aimed somewhere else.

Rank 7 Cosmic

Water at this rank can flood an entire area or lock it permanently under ice.

Default Ability: Absolute Zero (Permanent) Frozen conditions from your Water spells can only be ended by Fire Magic that exceeds your cast successes, and a Frozen target cannot use Reactions.

After the water passes, all affected zones become difficult terrain as water and debris remain. Defend: Strength + Athletics.

Dice Pool: Intelligence + Magic + Water dots

Effect: A massive glacier rises and fills the target zone entirely. Targets inside when it forms are Frozen and Pinned until the ice is destroyed or melted. Fire Magic reduces the glacier's Vitality by the Fire caster's successes per turn applied. Upkeep: 6.

A zone becomes a glacier all at once.

Air Magic

Air Magic is the school of wind, speed, and precision. Its spells excel at ranged attacks, movement disruption, and battlefield control through gusts and visibility reduction. Air assigns mana to the attack roll only. It carries elemental advantages against Water and Fire and is weakest against Earth and Nature.

Elemental interactions: +2 against Water, +1 against Fire, -1 against Nature, -2 against Earth.

Rank 1 Core

Air at this rank is a gust and a nudge. The caster can move air with intent and begin shaping where things fly.

Default Ability: Light Step (Permanent) Treat every fall as one vertical zone shorter when working out falling damage.

A targeted push of air with real force behind it.

Rank 2 Terrestrial

Earth Magic is the school of stone, endurance, and terrain control. Its spells build barriers, reshape the battlefield, and deliver heavy damage. Earth assigns mana to the damage roll only. It carries elemental advantages against Air and Water and is weakest against Nature and Fire.

Elemental interactions: +2 against Air, +1 against Water, -1 against Fire, -2 against Nature.

Shade Magic

Shade Magic is the school of darkness, mana drain, and corruption. It damages living targets, buffs undead, obscures vision, and drains mana directly from a target's pool. It cannot harm undead through a standard Shade attack. Exceptions require specific spells that name them. Shade assigns mana to the damage roll only.

Mana Bleed: Many Shade spells leave living targets losing mana at every Upkeep Phase (see How Casting Works).

Ethereal Shade, available at higher ranks, locks a target to their base Mana recovery rate with no damage bonus applied, regardless of how much damage they have taken.

Shade Magic has no elemental interactions with the five elemental schools. Its interactions are tied to living and undead targets.

Part Two: Combination Magic

Prerequisites and Dot Cap

Every combination spell states the minimum dots required in each school. The dot cap is the lowest rating among all schools involved. If you have 5 dots in Fire but only 2 in Earth, a Fire plus Earth combination is capped as though both schools were at 2. The combination spell's rank cannot exceed that cap regardless of individual school ratings.

Learning Combination Spells

A combination spell can be taken for free when gaining a new dot in one of its required schools, if that dot satisfies the prerequisites. Instead of taking one of the two standard options for that dot, the player takes the combination spell. This is the only way to learn a combination spell for free. Otherwise a combination spell costs Rank x2 XP for each school it combines, where Rank is the dots it requires in each school.

Mana Cost and Upkeep

A combination spell costs mana equal to the total dots it requires: the number of schools multiplied by the dots required in each. A two-school spell requires 2 dots in each school, 4 dots in all, and costs 4 mana. A three-school spell requires 3 dots in each, 9 in all, and costs 9 mana. Upkeep is always the mana cost minus 1.

Combination Spells in Artifacts and Implements

A combination spell imbued in an artifact or implement does not require the user to meet dot prerequisites. The item is treated as having the required dots built into it. The user simply provides the mana.

Creation Magic

Creation Magic is the single combination of all seven casting schools at 7 dots each. It is the only way to bring new autonomous life into the world, and it can be the work of many people together. Its full rules are at the end of this part.

A Miracle Shared

Creation Magic does not have to come from one caster, and it does not have to be paid all at once. Throughout history it has manifested through the combined efforts of many people. A circle of mages might each play their part, one shaping the body, another the mind, another the spark that holds it together. It does not always happen on purpose. Thousands of years ago, the people of a town believed a god lived in the mountain beside them. Generation after generation, their prayers, their belief, and the ambient magic of the land gathered in that mountain until the god they imagined manifested and took up residence there. It lives there still.

Benevolent and Malevolent

Creation Magic answers whatever is poured into it. It has made benevolent beings who protect the people who imagined them, and it has made evil ones. Every daemon in existence was given life by the combined fears of people. What a people dread long enough and deeply enough can become real.

Creation Is Not Conjuration

A conjuration is mana shaped into a temporary form. It needs upkeep, it ends when the upkeep stops or the caster is Incapacitated, and it can be dispelled. Creation Magic is different. What it makes exists on its own the moment it is complete. It needs no upkeep, it does not end when its creators fall, and it cannot be dispelled, because it is no longer a spell. It is part of the world.

Autonomous Life

Creation Magic is the only way to make fully autonomous life. Soul Summoning can create an entity that follows its summoner's commands, but that entity has no will of its own. A being made through Creation Magic thinks, chooses, and acts for itself.

A created being is not bound to obey those who made it. Anyone who wants its help has to earn it, ask for it, or make a pact with it, the same as with any other autonomous being. A created being can learn and use all seven casting schools, combination magic, and anything else a person can learn. The player and the Storyteller build a created being together as a new character.

1 to 2 successes: a simple object, material, or plant life

5 to 6 successes: an autonomous being with its own mind and will

How Circle Magic Is Learned

Circle magic disciplines appear under Magical Knowledges on the character sheet alongside the seven casting schools. They follow the same XP cost structure. They do not follow the same seven-rank spell list format. Each discipline has its own rules for what the practitioner can accomplish at different dot ratings, and those rules are listed under each discipline's entry.

The Disciplines of Circle Magic

Circle Magic is not a school of direct casting. It is a meta-discipline - a body of knowledge that allows a practitioner to capture, store, transfer, and reshape magical effects that other schools produce. A mage with Circle Magic dots can write what they know into the world in ways that persist beyond their own attention, and can interact with phenomena that raw casting cannot reach.

Circle Magic replaces the Magic ability in any Circle Magic roll. Where a casting school uses Intelligence + Magic + school dots, a circle of that spell uses Intelligence + Circle Magic + school dots. A Circle Magic roll that does not draw on a casting school uses Intelligence + Circle Magic. The successes from that roll are committed at the moment the circle or scroll or contract is made. They do not change after creation.

Circle Magic is learned separately from casting schools and has its own dot progression on the character sheet. A character does not need to know any particular school to study Circle Magic, but many of its disciplines only become truly powerful when combined with a deep knowledge of other schools.

The disciplines below represent the major branches of study within Circle Magic. A practitioner may pursue any combination of them. Some practitioners specialize deeply in one form; others take a broader approach.

Scroll Magic

Scroll Magic is the foundational discipline of Circle Magic and the one most practitioners learn first. A scroll is a prepared vessel for a single spell, committed at creation and released later by anyone who can provide the mana.

Creating a Scroll

To create a scroll, the caster must know the spell they wish to encode and have at least as many Circle Magic dots as the spell's rank. They draw the appropriate circle on a suitable medium - paper, cloth, stone, skin - channeling both the spell's school and the Circle Magic framework simultaneously.

The caster rolls Intelligence + Circle Magic + the spell's school dots. The successes achieved are locked into the scroll at creation. These successes serve as the spell's outcome when the scroll is later released. The caster does not roll again at release. For damage spells, the scroll's creator writes the damage type and notes the success count on the scroll itself.

Mana cost at creation: the creator spends the spell's full mana cost at the time of creation, plus 1 additional mana per Circle Magic dot involved as the framework cost.

Releasing a Scroll

Anyone holding a scroll can release it by spending the spell's mana cost from their own pool. They do not need to know the spell's school. The scroll activates using the success count locked in at creation. The scroll is consumed upon release.

A scroll cannot be released in the same turn it is drawn out. Drawing a scroll takes a standard action. Releasing it takes a standard action on the following turn.

Scroll Limits

A character can carry prepared scrolls equal to their Circle Magic dots. Scrolls kept in inventory beyond combat reach take no limit slot. A scroll created by someone else functions normally. Scrolls degrade after one month if not stored in a proper Circle Satchel (a minor magical item worth noting on equipment lists).

Divination

Divination uses Circle Magic to read information from time, place, and connection rather than imposing effects on the world. A diviner asks questions of the fabric of things.

Basic Divination

With 1-2 Circle Magic dots, a practitioner can read recent impressions from objects and places. Roll Intelligence + Circle Magic. Each success reveals one fact about the most recent significant event tied to the object or location. The scene is the minimum time frame; exact timing requires more successes.

Advanced Divination

With 3-5 dots, a practitioner can trace connections between people and objects across distances. They can ask directional questions (which way, how far) or temporal ones (when did this happen, how long ago). The difficulty scales with distance and time elapsed. A question about something that happened yesterday in the same city is trivial. A question about something that happened three decades ago in another country requires a very high success count.

Prophetic Divination

With 6-7 dots, a practitioner can look forward. Future divination is never exact. The GM provides images, sensations, and symbolic impressions rather than facts. Probability is not certainty, and the future shifts based on choices made. What a 6-dot diviner sees is the most likely outcome from the current moment forward; a 7-dot diviner sees several branching paths and their relative likelihood. Neither sees inevitability.

Future divination costs 5 mana per attempt and requires at least 30 uninterrupted minutes outside of combat.

Summoning Magic

Summoning Magic brings entities from other planes or states of being into the physical world. It is divided into two branches with different prerequisites, rules, and risks.

Fiend Summoning

Fiend Summoning calls creatures that already exist in the world. A summoner gains a fiend by catching it, making a pact with it, or buying it. When a fiend is not needed it lives in a ranch or another place where it can live happily.

Each fiend is tied to a device etched with its soul mark. The summoner carries that device and uses it to call the fiend to the battlefield from wherever it is in the world. The device can take any form, such as a ball or a coin. In the current day it is most popular to keep them on cards, and fiends are bought, sold, and traded this way. In common use these creatures are called SumMonz.

Fiends can use the five main elemental casting schools (Fire, Water, Air, Earth, and Nature) and Battle Aura.

Soul Summoning

Soul Summoning covers beings that do not fall into the fiend category. These beings live in a demi-plane or a similar place and are summoned from there. They include fey beings, undead creatures, devils, and even a completely new being of the player's own design.

A summoner can make a pact with an existing being that already has its own autonomy, such as a fey or a devil. A summoner can also create an entity of their own. Without Creation Magic that entity is not fully autonomous life. It is an entity that follows the summoner's commands.

Soul summoned beings can use all seven casting schools, including combination magic. This is the main system difference between the two branches of summoning.

The SumMonz League

Most people, even those in the SumMonz League, do not know or care about the distinction between fiends and soul summoned beings. What they do care about is that elemental creatures are affected differently by elemental attacks. A water sprite deals more damage to a fire sprite, and so on, following the Elemental Advantages.

Using Your SumMonz

Cards in Combat: Circle magic cannot be started in combat, but a card etched with a soul mark is a finished product, so you can summon from one mid-fight. Without the card you cannot summon that SumMonz.

Summoning: During either Move or Use Item phase, spend mana equal to the SumMonz's rank to call it to an empty zone next to you. It counts as using a belt item and follows the same AP costs. Calling it back to its card is free.

How Many: You can have 1 SumMonz on the field at 1 to 3 dots of Summoning Magic, 2 at 4 to 6 dots, and 3 at 7. You can carry up to 6 cards at once.

Where They Live: Fiends you are not carrying live at a ranch or another place where they can live happily. Soul summoned beings live in the Dreeming or the Seeming and are called from there.

Commanding: A summoned SumMonz acts on your turn, and commanding it costs you no action. It moves and attacks in the same phases you do, using its own pools.

Support: You can give your SumMonz AP and mana the same way you would give them to an ally, and any healing that works on a character works on a SumMonz.

Rank Limit: A SumMonz's rank can never be raised above your Summoning Magic dots. One that outranks you, such as a gift or a fiend whose summoner died, may refuse commands. A Composure + Summoning Magic roll against its rank x2 brings it in line for the scene.

Knocked Out: A SumMonz that is Incapacitated returns to its card and cannot be summoned again until after a long rest.

Elements: Elemental Advantages apply to SumMonz exactly as they do to spells and conjurations.

SumMonz Stats

Every SumMonz is built from its rank (R), from 1 at F to 7 at S, using the same numbers as every creature in the Bestiary (Chapter 15).

Attack and Defense: 2R + 2 dice each.

Damage: a damage pool of R + 2 dice, rolled like a weapon's. Add the attack's excess successes and compare the total to the target's thresholds.

Thresholds: BDT R + 2, LDT BDT x2, ADT BDT x3.

Health: R + 2 Bashing boxes, R + 1 Lethal boxes, and R Aggravated boxes.

Mana: Pool 3R, M/T R + 1. A SumMonz pays for its own techniques from its own pool.

Movement: 1 zone per turn. A flying SumMonz moves at double.

Techniques: Up to 4, chosen from spells of a rank no higher than its own, rolled with its own Attack pool. Fiends draw from Fire, Water, Air, Earth, and Nature Magic and Battle Aura. Soul summoned beings draw from all seven casting schools and combination magic.

Evolving

While a SumMonz is on the field, you can pour more mana into it to push it into a stronger form. This counts toward your Max Mana Per Turn, and an evolved form may rise above your Summoning Magic dots while it lasts.

Second Form: Spend mana equal to the second form's rank. The SumMonz rises 1 rank, its stats rise to match, and it gains its form technique.

Third Form: From its second form, spend mana equal to the third form's rank. It rises another rank, grows to fill a 2x2 zone area, and gains its third form technique.

Holding a Form: An evolved form burns its own mana each Upkeep Phase: 1 for the second form, 2 for the third. When its mana runs out, it reverts to its first form. If you call it back and do not summon it again within 2 turns, it also reverts.

Getting and Growing SumMonz

Catching: Weaken a wild fiend until all of its Lethal boxes are filled, then press a blank card to it and roll Wisdom + Summoning Magic against its rank x2. On a success its soul mark is etched into the card. Blank cards are sold by rank and priced like any item of that rank (Chapter 14).

Pacts: A fiend or soul summoned being that can talk may agree to a pact instead. Play it out as a social scene, usually Persuasion or Diplomacy against its Composure. It will want something in return.

Buying and Trading: SumMonz cards are bought, sold, and traded like any item of their rank (Chapter 14). Whoever holds the card holds the SumMonz.

Growing: Raise a SumMonz's rank by spending XP equal to the new rank x3, never above your Summoning Magic dots. Each new rank can add one technique, up to 4.

Creating: A soul summoned being of your own design starts at rank 1 and grows the same way. Without Creation Magic it follows your commands but has no will of its own.

Example: Cinder is an Ember Fox fiend (Fire and Battle Aura) of rank D (3): Attack 8, Defense 8, Damage 5, BDT 5, Health 5 / 4 / 3, Mana 9, M/T 4, Movement 1. Summoning Cinder costs 3 mana. Pouring in 4 more evolves it into Blazetail (rank 4), and 5 more after that into Pyre Vixen (rank 5), who fills a 2x2 area.

Summoner's Sheet: Track up to 6 SumMonz, with all three forms, on the Summoner's Sheet at the back of this book.

Teleportation

Teleportation Magic uses circles drawn at both origin and destination to create an instantaneous passage between two prepared points. Unlike Shadow Step (which uses existing darkness), Teleportation Magic creates its own passage.

Paired Circles

The standard use of Teleportation requires the caster to have previously prepared a destination circle and have it active. A destination circle takes 10 minutes to inscribe, costs Circle Magic dots in mana, and persists for up to a month without maintenance. The caster keeps a resonance token - a small inscribed object - that lets them call the circle when opening the transit.

Activating a transit: roll Intelligence + Circle Magic. The transit opens on 1 success and remains open for 1 turn per success, allowing any number of willing travelers to pass through. The mana cost is 2 per Circle Magic dot. A caster may only have active destination circles equal to their Circle Magic dots at one time.

One-Way Teleportation

With 5+ Circle Magic dots, a caster can force a teleportation without a prepared destination circle, selecting any location they have personally visited and can visualize clearly. This is harder: the roll loses 2 dice, the mana cost doubles, and the transit only allows one traveler (the caster). On a failure, the teleportation does not occur and the mana is spent. On a botch (all dice fail), the transit occurs but deposits the caster somewhere adjacent to rather than at the intended destination.

Teleportation to an Unknown Location

Teleporting to a location one has never seen requires a combination of Divination and Teleportation (minimum 5 dots in Circle Magic and at least 1 success on a prior Divination roll about the location). This is extremely difficult and the margin for error is significant. This is the purview of the most experienced practitioners only.

Contract Magic

Contract Magic makes binding agreements - magical compacts with real teeth. A contract created through Circle Magic is enforced not by trust but by force. The moment both parties sign or otherwise accept the terms, the magic takes hold.

Crafting a Contract

A Circle Magic practitioner draws up a contract by scribing its terms within a circle. The terms must be specific and possible to verify. Vague language works against the writer: the magic interprets terms literally, and ambiguity is resolved against the party who drafted the contract. The caster rolls Intelligence + Circle Magic to set the contract's power level. More successes mean a tighter binding with harsher consequences for breach.

A contract must specify: the parties involved, the terms each party agrees to, the duration, and the consequence for breach. The consequence for breach must be proportionate in some form - a contract cannot specify death for a minor obligation, though what counts as proportionate is a matter for the GM and the players to navigate.

Signing and Activation

A contract activates the moment all named parties have accepted its terms. Acceptance can be a signature, a spoken agreement in the circle's presence, or any other clear act of consent. Coerced signatures are valid but contested: a party who signed under direct magical compulsion may challenge the contract before a higher power (a guild, a court, a more powerful Circle mage).

Once activated, the contract is a persistent magical effect. Both parties feel its presence as a faint but unmistakable sensation whenever they take an action that might be relevant to the terms.

Breach and Consequences

If a party breaches the terms of an active contract, the magic triggers. Mild contracts impose conditions (Disadvantaged on relevant rolls, recurring minor damage). Strong contracts (created by high-success casters) can be genuinely dangerous - the breaching party may take Lethal or even Aggravated damage, lose mana, or suffer persistent conditions until the breach is resolved. The magic does not care about intent; it cares about the letter of the terms.

A contract can be dissolved by mutual consent of all parties. It can also be broken by destroying the physical medium on which it was written, though this requires more successes on a Circle Magic roll than were used to create it.

Devil Contracts

Devil contracts are a specific and particularly binding form of Contract Magic. A contract with a devil follows all the rules above but adds several complications. Devils negotiate their terms very carefully and always seek the version of events most favorable to their interpretation. They will not sign a contract they cannot fulfill, but they will sign contracts designed to make fulfillment advantageous on their terms.

A devil that breaches its contract suffers consequences in proportion to the breach, as with any contract, but these consequences extend to their standing in their home plane. Devils take contract obligations seriously for this reason.

A human party that breaches a devil contract typically suffers worse consequences than a comparable mundane contract would impose, because devils draft their contracts with that asymmetry built in. Reading every clause carefully before signing is not just good advice - it is a survival skill.

Binding Rituals

Binding Rituals use Circle Magic to affix magical effects, spirits, or entities to objects, locations, or thresholds. Unlike contracts, bindings do not require consent from the target. They are imposed by force or prepared in advance.

Object Binding

An object can be bound with any spell effect the caster knows. The object stores the effect until a trigger condition is met. The trigger must be specific and detectable: "when touched by a person not carrying my resonance token," "when this seal is broken," "when someone speaks the word carved on the lid." Abstract triggers do not work.

Binding an effect to an object requires the caster to have both the spell and at least Circle Magic dots equal to the spell's rank. They cast the spell into the object rather than into the world, rolling Intelligence + Circle Magic + the spell's school dots. The successes are stored with the binding. The bound effect can trigger once (expendable binding) or repeatedly at the cost of a mana charge per trigger (persistent binding, which requires the object to have a mana reservoir).

Location Binding

A location can be bound with effects that trigger on entry, exit, or condition. Location bindings are used to create traps, wards, and persistent environmental effects. A Zone-scale location binding covers one zone. Larger areas require higher Circle Magic dots and proportionally more successes.

Location bindings persist for a month per Circle Magic dot used in the creation. A permanent location binding requires at least 6 Circle Magic dots and a very high success count. Permanent bindings are rare and usually notable features of the places that contain them.

Entity Binding

Entity binding captures an entity and forces it into an object or location. It requires a contest between the binder's Circle Magic roll and the entity's resistance and costs 5 mana per Circle Magic dot.

Once bound, the entity is contained. It is aware of its imprisonment. It retains its personality and goals. This is worth remembering when dealing with pacted creatures who were bound rather than persuaded.

Ward Magic

Wards are protective or alerting circles placed on zones, objects, or thresholds. They are a specialized subset of location bindings focused specifically on detecting intrusion, deflecting effects, or creating contained areas of magical influence.

Alert Wards

An alert ward triggers silently and notifies the creator when a condition is met. The notification is a mental impression - a directional sense of activation, the general nature of what triggered it, and a rough sense of distance. Alert wards do not require high success counts; even 1 success on the creation roll produces a functional ward. Higher successes give more precise information on trigger.

Alert wards cost 1 mana per Circle Magic dot to create and persist for a week per success.

Defensive Wards

A defensive ward interposes itself between a protected zone or object and incoming magical effects. When a spell targets something inside a defensive ward, the ward's creation successes act as a resistance DT against that spell. The ward absorbs hits by losing successes; when reduced to 0, it breaks.

Defensive wards cost 3 mana per Circle Magic dot to create. They persist until broken or until a month has passed. They can be recharged by spending mana equal to their original creation cost, which restores their success pool.

Exclusion Wards

An exclusion ward creates an invisible line that certain types of entities or effects cannot cross. A ward against undead, against fey, against fire effects, or against a specific named entity requires the caster to have at least as many Circle Magic dots as the category's power level. Low-level undead can be warded off by a 2-dot exclusion; a named ancient vampire requires 6+.

Exclusion wards do not damage entities; they simply prevent passage. A powerful enough entity can push through an exclusion ward by spending Willpower and succeeding on a contested roll against the ward's creation successes.

Augmentation

Augmentation is the circle magic of building magic into weapons so that their attacks can carry it. A weapon can only receive an augmentation if its description lists an augment slot.

An augmentation can be made from any of the seven casting magics, or even from other circle magics. Using an augmentation during an attack still requires spending mana. Each augmentation has its own parameters, set when it is made, that determine what it can and cannot do. An augmented attack follows the same mana assignment rules as any attack carrying a school's energy (see Imbuing Attacks).

Spatial Magic

Spatial Magic folds space so that a container holds far more than its outside size should allow. It is used most often to make magical storage implements: bags, chests, pouches, rings, and similar items.

Storage is counted per kind of object. The strength of the design, set by the Circle Magic dots put into it, decides how many of each kind of object it can hold. Each dot doubles the amount:

1 dot: 1 of each kind of object

2 dots: 2 of each kind of object

3 dots: 4 of each kind of object

4 dots: 8 of each kind of object

5 dots: 16 of each kind of object

6 dots: 32 of each kind of object

7 dots: 64 of each kind of object

A storage implement made at 3 dots can hold 4 swords, 4 potions, 4 loaves of bread, and 4 of every other kind of object at the same time. The creator chooses the strength of the design, up to their own Circle Magic dots.

Time Magic

Time Magic is the deepest and most demanding branch of Circle Magic. It is not fully expressible within the conventional dot scale, and serious time magic lies beyond what any practitioner currently active has reliably achieved. However, it is an area of active study, and the foundations are accessible.

Minor Temporal Effects (4-5 dots)

A practitioner with 4-5 Circle Magic dots can produce minor temporal effects: slowing or accelerating the passage of time within a small object (aging a fruit, preserving a body, slowing the burning of a candle). These effects do not affect living creatures and do not change the past or future. They manipulate the rate at which time passes for inert things within a prepared circle.

Temporal Stasis (6 dots)

At 6 Circle Magic dots, a practitioner can create a Temporal Stasis circle that suspends time within a small zone or object entirely. Anything inside the circle stops aging, stops changing, stops experiencing duration. The effect persists until the circle is broken. This is used to preserve things perfectly: wounds, artifacts, living creatures placed voluntarily inside. A creature placed in stasis does not experience the time that passes while stasis is active.

Temporal Stasis cost: 10 mana to create, 2 mana per day to maintain for a persistent circle. Breaking a stasis circle (from outside) requires meeting or exceeding its creation successes on a Circle Magic roll.

Temporal Observation (7 dots)

At 7 Circle Magic dots, a practitioner can observe the past of a specific location - not through Divination's impressions, but through direct temporal viewing. They see events as they occurred, with clarity proportional to success count and proximity to the present. One success per decade of distance is the rough scale.

Temporal Observation cannot change the past. It does not allow communication with the past. It is purely a window. The practitioner sees and hears; they cannot interact.

True Time Manipulation (Beyond 7 Dots)

The ability to directly alter past events, travel through time, or bring future states into the present is theoretically within the reach of Circle Magic but lies beyond the standard dot scale. Whether such a thing is truly possible - and what it would cost - remains one of the open questions in the magical world. Practitioners who pursue this path report that the circles required are of extraordinary complexity, the mana costs staggering, and the philosophical implications not easily survived.

If a player wishes to pursue time travel as a long-term goal for their character, discuss with your GM. It is a valid aspiration. The path to it is not charted here because it has not yet been charted at all.

Circle Magic and Combat

No circle magic can be initiated during combat. Any technique that requires setup, preparation, drawn lines, or spoken incantation taking longer than one instant cannot be started once initiative has been called. A practitioner who was mid-ritual when combat begins has their ritual interrupted unless the Storyteller rules otherwise.

The exception is finished products. A scroll, contract, ward trigger, or bound item that was completed before the fight started can be activated as normal. Activating a finished product follows the product's own rules for speed and cost, not the rules for ritual preparation.
