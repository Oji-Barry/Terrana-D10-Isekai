Introduction

Not every fighter in Terrana casts spells. Sword schools, fight pits, hunting lodges, and drill yards all pass down their own ways of fighting, and a master of one is as dangerous as any mage. A Combat Style is one of those traditions: a single form of combat, trained from the first lesson to mastery.

Each Style covers one form, such as short blades, polearms, or boxing. Every dot teaches something new, in a fight or outside one. The knife fighter learns to skin game before learning to throw a blade, and the wrestler learns to work a crowd long before the piledriver. Styles are built to pair with Battle Aura, which pours fighting spirit into whatever Style a character practices.

How Styles Work

Ranks: A Style has seven dots, named on the Rank Scale used across the character sheet: Elementary, Intermediate, Advanced, Saint, Royal, Imperial, and Divine (F through S).

Pools: Every Style lists a Primary pool, used for its attacks, and a Secondary pool, used to defend or support. Both are an Attribute plus an Ability.

What Each Dot Gives: Every rank has a Default Ability and two Options. When you buy the dot, you gain the Default Ability and pick Option A or Option B for free. The option you did not pick is not lost: you can buy it later for Rank x2 XP, without raising the Style again.

Use: Every entry is marked Combat, Outside combat, or Both, the same as spells.

Your Dots: When an entry says "your dots," it means your dots in that Style. A bonus to your own rolls or an ally's rolls equals your dots. A bonus to BDT equals half your dots, rounded up. Penalties a technique puts on an enemy never scale: they stay at the flat amount written.

Learning Styles

At Creation: In Step 10, your Physical column decides how many Combat Style dots you spend: 7 for Affinity, 5 for Standard, or 3 for Aversion. Spread them across any Styles you like. The Golden Rule applies, so no Style can go above 5 dots at creation. Mark every Style you take in this step with a P for Primary.

Freebie Points: Combat Style dots cost 4 Freebie Points each. A new Style bought with freebies is marked with an S for Secondary.

XP: A Primary Style costs the new dot x5. Any other Style costs the new dot x6. The option you did not pick at a rank costs Rank x2 XP.

Divine and the God Titles: Reaching the Divine rank makes you one of the finest practitioners alive, but titles such as Sword God or Water God are not ranks of a Style. They are won and held (Chapter 5).

Combat Terms

Zones: Only one creature or object can be in a zone. Melee attacks reach adjacent zones, so "all adjacent zones" means everyone right next to you. A charge or leap always ends in an empty zone.

One Attack Roll: A technique that hits several targets, or the same target several times, still makes a single attack roll. Each target, and each strike, defends against that roll separately.

Counter: When your defense roll beats an attack, a Counter lets you strike back using that same defense roll as your attack roll. The attacker defends normally, and has to be within reach of your weapon.

Armor Penetration: BDT is Fortitude + Armor, plus any magic that raises it. Fortitude here includes Resilience and any other bonus to Fortitude. Armor Penetration lowers the Armor part of the target's BDT by the amount listed, for your hit only, and their LDT and ADT drop with it. Full Armor Penetration ignores the whole Armor part. It never lowers Fortitude.

Magic Penetration: Works the same way on the magic part of a BDT: points added by spells, enchantments, or magical gear.

Stacking Penetration: Penetration from different sources adds together, but neither kind can take its part of a BDT below 0.

Lowering Armor: Some techniques lower a target's Armor for the rest of the scene. That damages their gear, so it helps everyone attacking them, unlike Armor Penetration, which only applies to your own hit.

Called Shots: Aiming at a specific limb, weapon, or vital spot costs -2 dice on the attack roll. Its effect only happens if the attack hits.

Range: Melee weapons hit adjacent zones, or one zone farther with Reach. Thrown weapons go up to double your Strength in zones. Bows, guns, and ranged spells can hit anywhere you have line of sight to; half cover gives -2 dice (Chapter 14).

Conditions: Bleeding, Prone, Rooted, and Grappled work as written in the Conditions rules. A Bleeding effect from a Style has a rating equal to your dots. When a technique makes a target lose AP on their next turn, that is how a stun works in Terrana.

Combat Actions: Anyone can take one of these in the Action Phase instead of an attack. The first four are contests: you roll, the target rolls, and you need more successes. They reach adjacent zones.

Shove: Strength + Athletics against the target's Strength + Athletics. If you win, they are pushed 1 zone straight away from you into an empty zone. With no empty zone behind them, they stay put.

Trip: Dexterity + Athletics against the target's Dexterity + Acrobatics. If you win, they are knocked Prone.

Grapple: Strength + Martial Arts against the target's Dodge or Strength + Athletics, whichever is better. If you win, they are Grappled.

Disarm: Your attack pool against the target's defense. If you win, their weapon lands in an adjacent zone.

Hide: In or next to cover, roll Dexterity + Stealth. Everyone against you rolls Perception + Awareness. Beat them all and you are hidden until you attack or someone finds you.

Total Defense: Give up your attack. Add 2 dice to every defense roll until your next turn.

Take Cover: In or next to cover, get down behind it. Until your next turn, add 1 die to every defense roll behind half cover (brush, crates), or 3 dice behind full cover (walls, boulders).

Aim: Give up your attack and pick a target in line of sight. Add 2 dice to your next ranged attack against them, made before the end of your next turn.

Extra Attacks: Once per round, a Style attack or maneuver can be made more than once by spending AP before the first roll. With 3 dots in that Style, 1 AP buys 1 extra attack. With 5 dots, 3 AP buys 2 extra attacks. With 7 dots, 6 AP (allies can give AP) buys 3 extra attacks. Every extra attack is the same attack you chose, each one is rolled on its own, and each target defends against it as normal. Each attack can pick its own target in range, and each hit rolls its own damage.

Styles and Battle Aura

Battle Aura is built to work with every Style. Its attack techniques roll the Attribute + Ability of the Style you are fighting with, plus your dots in that Style. With its second dot, Battle Aura gives Aura Edge, an attuned ability that adds Armor Penetration and Magic Penetration to your weapon and unarmed attacks. Aura Edge stacks with any Armor Penetration your Style gives (Chapter 13).

Quickcut Style

Short Blades

Small blades: get close, cut what matters, and be gone before the other side reacts.

Covers: daggers, knives (skinning knives and work knives too), stilettos, short swords, throwing knives.

Primary Dexterity + Melee
Secondary Dexterity + Stealth

Rank 1 Elementary

Default AbilitySkinner's HandsOutside combat

You're skilled at field-dressing, skinning, butchering, and knife work (whittling, cutting rope, scraping hides). Add your dots to rolls to skin or harvest a creature. Each success also saves one extra usable part (hide, sinew, horn, or gland).

Option AQuick HandsCombat

Drawing or swapping to a short blade costs no AP. Add your dots to initiative if you start combat with one in hand.

Option BFind the GapCombat

Add your dots to attack rolls against a target that hasn't acted yet this round.

Rank 2 Intermediate

Default AbilityThrowing EdgeBoth

You can throw any short blade using this Style's Primary pool, at a target up to double your Strength in zones away. It works outside a fight too, to hunt small game or cut a rope.

Option ATwin GripCombat

Wield a short blade in each hand. When you attack with both, add your dots to that one attack roll.

Option BPin and RunCombat

When a thrown blade hits, spend 1 AP to pin the target's clothing or foot. They're Rooted until the end of their next turn.

Rank 3 Advanced

Default AbilityHidden SteelBoth

You get one extra Belt slot (slot 4), which can only hold short blades. Drawing from it costs no AP. Add your dots to rolls to keep a blade hidden when you're searched.

Option AOpening CutCombat

On a hit, spend 1 AP to give the target Bleeding, at a rating equal to your dots.

Option BSlip AwayCombat

After you make a melee attack, move 1 zone for free.

Rank 4 Saint

Default AbilityButcher's EyeOutside combat

One look tells you how a creature is put together and where it's weak. Add your dots to rolls to cut out poison, treat a wound with a knife, or track something you've wounded.

Option AWeak PointCombat

Once per round, a short blade hit has Armor Penetration equal to half your dots.

Option BDancer's StepCombat

Add your dots to defense rolls against an adjacent enemy you've hit this round.

Rank 5 Royal

Default AbilityFan of KnivesCombat

One thrown attack can target up to three creatures within throwing range, using one attack roll.

Option ASecond StrikeCombat

If your attack roll beats the target's defense by 3 or more successes, add your dots to the damage.

Option BGhost StepCombat

Once per round, when your defense roll beats an adjacent attack, make a Counter with a short blade, then move 1 zone for free.

Rank 6 Imperial

Default AbilityUnseen ApproachBoth

Add your dots to Stealth rolls. Your first hit on a target that hasn't noticed you counts as meeting the next-higher damage threshold.

Option ASevering StrikeCombat

A called shot that hits a limb gives the target -2 dice on rolls using that limb for the rest of the scene.

Option BBreath CutCombat

Spend 2 AP: on a hit, the target also loses 2 AP on their next turn.

Rank 7 Divine

Default AbilityEvery Edge Is MineBoth

Anything with an edge or point (a glass shard, a broken sword, a sharpened spoon) counts as a short blade for you, with a Rating equal to your dots.

Option ADeath of a Thousand CutsCombat

Spend 3 AP: one attack roll against every enemy in all adjacent zones. Each one you hit also gets Bleeding, at a rating equal to your dots.

Option BThe Last MercyCombat

Once per story, a hit on a target at their -4 wound penalty or worse automatically meets their ADT.
