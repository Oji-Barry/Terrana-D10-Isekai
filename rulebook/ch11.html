Introduction

Willpower, Fate, Sanity, and Mana sit outside the normal attribute and ability system. Willpower, Fate, and Sanity are never bought with Freebie Points or XP; they are built and lost through play. Mana is the exception: it can be bought, but only at a steep price. Together these four stats describe the deeper texture of who your character is and what the world asks of them across a campaign.

Every character starts with 5 dots each in Willpower, Fate, and Sanity (Willpower may start at 4 or 6, depending on your highs), and 1 dot of Mana.

Dots and Boxes

Willpower, Fate, and Sanity are each tracked with a row of 10 dots and, beneath it, a row of 10 boxes. The dots are your rating: it can never go above 10. The boxes are a running record of what has happened to that stat in play. They do not reset when you rest, after a session, or at the end of a story chapter. They only change when new marks are added.

A box can be in one of three states: blank, checked, or marked with an X.

Checks: Gains, growth, and healing are marked with checks, filling the row from left to right.

X's: Damage, spending, and loss are marked with X's, filling the row from right to left.

A Mixed Row: When the row is full of checks and X's together, a new check replaces the X nearest the checks, and a new X replaces the check nearest the X's. Each side pushes against the other.

A Full Row: When all 10 boxes are checks, gain 1 dot. When all 10 are X's, lose 1 dot. Either way, erase every mark in the row and start fresh.

Dots can only change through a full row (and a few Merits, Flaws, and Storyteller awards). None of the three can ever go above 10 or below 1. Like an attribute, the last dot can never be lost: a full row of X's at 1 dot is erased, but the dot stays.

Willpower

Willpower is your character's mental and emotional resilience. It is the resource you spend when you need to push past your limits, and the health track you protect when someone tries to break you with words instead of weapons.

Starting Willpower

Willpower starts at 5 dots. If both of your highs are Vices, it starts at 4; if both are Virtues, it starts at 6; one of each leaves it at 5. The Iron Will Merit adds 1 dot per dot of the Merit, up to 3. After creation, moving on the scales never changes your Willpower dots.

Spending Willpower

You can spend 1 Willpower per turn. Spending it takes no action. Mark 1 X and choose one:

Push: Before a roll, add 3 dice to it.

Reroll: After a roll, reroll up to 3 failed dice.

You can keep spending even when your row is mostly X's. If the row fills with X's, you lose a Willpower dot and erase the row. Some Battle Aura techniques at Rank 4 and higher can be paid with Willpower instead of mana (Chapter 13). That payment is separate from this once-per-turn spend, and each point still marks an X.

Taking Willpower Damage

Willpower damage marks X's, the same as spending. It comes from:

Social combat: Willpower is the health track for social combat (see below).

Acting against your scales: Any violation at a Low Virtue or Low Vice, and minor violations at a High position, cost Willpower (Chapter 7, Part 2).

Small scale shifts: When the Storyteller moves one of your scales a short way.

Social Combat

In social combat, the attacker rolls a Social Attribute plus a relevant Ability. The defender rolls Willpower plus Composure. Each success the attacker has over the defender deals 1 Willpower damage. How each side acts can earn bonus or penalty dice from their scales.

A social combat ends when either side fills their row with X's (losing a Willpower dot, or erasing the row at 1 dot), or when 5 or more damage is dealt in a single retort. A defender who rolls 5 or more successes over the attacker makes a Critical Comeback: the attacker loses momentum and the exchange ends in the defender's favor.

Regaining Willpower

Resting does not erase X's. Willpower is regained by earning checks, which push the X's back. The Storyteller awards checks when your character acts meaningfully on their Driving Factors or Trope, holds firm on what they believe at a real cost, or takes genuine time off to do things they love after a demanding job. A session where a character fully embodies their drive earns more than one where they only touched on it.

Fill the whole row with checks and you gain a Willpower dot.

Fate

Fate is your character's karmic weight in the world: how deeply they are woven into the story of Terrana and how much they can bend it. Where Willpower is for tackling the thing right in front of you, Fate is your ability to weave the world's story in your favor, and to stand against outcomes that seem preordained.

Fate is not spent. It is built and lost through how much influence your character has in the world.

How Much Fate

Player characters start at 5 dots simply by being reincarnates: arriving in this world from another is itself a significant karmic event. The average person has about 3. Kings and other figures of great worldly power typically have 8 or more. Death itself has a Fate of 10.

Building and Losing Fate

Checks: Making plans that succeed, striking and keeping deals, taking opportunities, forming ties with important people, and engaging with the forces that shape history.

X's: Plans that fail, broken deals, opportunities ignored, refusing the requests of powerful people, and losing a Fate contest.

A full row of checks gains a Fate dot; a full row of X's loses one. Fate can never fall below 1.

Bending the Story

When your character works with the flow of the world, asking it for something it has no reason to refuse, the Storyteller should generally let Fate carry the moment without a roll. A dying friend getting to see the flowers bloom one more time is a story the world is willing to tell.

When your character pushes against something with its own Fate, the two meet in a Fate contest.

Fate Contests

Both sides roll their Fate dots as a dice pool. The side with more successes gets its way; the defender wins ties. The winner marks a check and the loser marks an X.

Opposing Fate can be pooled. A character trying to stop a death rolls against Death's 10. A character trying to stop an arranged royal wedding rolls against the combined Fate of the two kingdoms behind it, which may be 16 dice or more. Challenging the groom to a duel instead is not a Fate contest at all; it is just a duel.

Fate is fickle. Pushing against forces far heavier than you is a quick way to lose it, and learning when Fate is worth testing is part of the game.

Sanity

Sanity is your character's connection to reality, their psychological stability, and their capacity to process what the world puts in front of them. Most of Terrana can be managed. Voidlings are different: their existence is hostile to the ordered mind, and parties that face them regularly need to look after each other.

Sanity damage accumulates differently from physical or social damage and heals differently. Lost Sanity changes who the character is, not just what they can do. Your Sanity dots are also your dice pool for resisting Voidling horror and other Void effects.

Taking Sanity Damage

Sanity damage marks X's. It comes from:

Severe violations at a High position: acting fundamentally against a High Virtue or High Vice (Chapter 7, Part 2).

Significant scale shifts: when the Storyteller moves a scale a long way.

The Void: exposure to Voidlings and Void phenomena, whatever happens to the body.

Resisting a compulsion: sustained resistance to a vampire's compulsion without resolution.

Some powers: a few Merits and spells cost Sanity to use.

Healing the Mind

Sanity heals by living life and having fun, marked with checks. What heals the mind varies from person to person, so this is intentionally broad: acting on your Driving Factors over time, spending real time with people you care about, or some form of therapy that fits the character. That might be an actual therapist, a fortune teller, a night drinking with friends, a hot spring, or a creative hobby. The Storyteller decides how many checks it earns based on how genuinely the character engaged, not just whether they went through the motions.

Losing Sanity Dots

Every character starts at 5 Sanity. When a full row of X's costs a dot, the player and Storyteller give the character a mental trauma shaped by what caused the loss. It may change how they interact with the world, and it grows more extreme the lower Sanity falls.

5 or higher: Stable. No trauma.

4: A phobia or lasting quirk tied to what caused the loss.

3: A serious trauma that shapes how the character acts and reacts.

2: A severe trauma that can take over in moments of stress.

1: The player loses control. The Storyteller runs the character until long-term care brings them back.

Sanity can never drop below 1. Like an attribute, losing the last dot would mean the character no longer exists.

Gaining Sanity Dots

A full row of checks gains a dot and a deeper, more resilient hold on reality. Recovering a dot can soften or remove a trauma. At 10, the character's grasp of reality is so complete that they no longer see the world the way ordinary people do. The player loses control, and the Storyteller runs the character.

Mana

Everything in Terrana carries mana: rocks, trees, insects, rivers, and the air between things. It is the fundamental substance of the world and the medium magic moves through. Anyone raised here has at least 1 dot from growing up surrounded by it. To someone who arrives with none, the same ambient mana is closer to poison.

Mana Dots

Mana is rated from 1 to 10 dots, the only trait with ten. Every character starts with 1. At creation it can only be raised with Freebie Points, at 7 points per dot, and the Golden Rule still applies. After creation, each dot costs the new dot x7 in XP. Mana counts toward your Level.

Mana Pool

Mana Pool = (Magic dots + Circle Magic dots + every Magical Knowledge dot) x Mana.

Every Magical Knowledge counts: casting schools, Battle Aura, the Circle Magic disciplines, and Heritage magic, including each Vampiric Power a vampire learns. For example, Magic 1, Circle Magic 4, Battle Aura 2, Summoning Magic 3, and Blood Sorcery 3 make 13, so with Mana 2 the pool is 26. Learning Celerity at 1 dot raises it to 28. With no dots in any of these, your pool is 0. Some items let other abilities, such as Alchemy, Attunement, or Implements, count too. The Mana Reservoir Merit and Mana Deficient Flaw adjust the pool.

Maximum Mana Per Turn

Max Mana Per Turn (M/T) = Fortitude + Mana.

This is the most mana you can safely spend in one turn, however large your pool is. Upkeep costs paid during the Upkeep Phase count toward it. Attuned effects and items hold part of it for as long as they are active, and some Arcane Enhancements lower it permanently.

Spending Mana

Mana pays for spells, techniques, and other magical effects. Mana can also enhance a roll at 1 die per mana, declared before rolling and assigned to the attack or the damage roll. Spending mana to boost anything is always limited to your dots in that magic or combat ability, or your M/T, whichever comes first.

Attuned and Committed Mana

Attuned: Artifacts, augmentations, and attuned default abilities hold part of your M/T while active. No further mana is needed to use them.

Committed: Persistent effects cost their upkeep (the spell's cost minus 1) every Upkeep Phase, and that upkeep counts toward your M/T.

Ending an attuned or committed effect returns that mana to your M/T during the following Upkeep Phase.

Running Dry

A character who spends their pool down to 0 drops unconscious and is Incapacitated until they recover mana. A character whose pool is 0 only because they have no dots in Magic or Circle Magic is not affected.

Mana Recovery

In combat you recover mana during each Upkeep Phase, based on your Mana dots, plus 1 if you took damage since your last turn. Recovery never takes you above your pool. Outside combat, a few minutes of rest refills the pool, so unless a party fights battle after battle without a break, they start every encounter full.

Mana Burn

You may choose to spend more than your M/T in a single turn, but every mana over the limit burns. Filling all of your Aggravated boxes through Mana Burn knocks you incapacitated immediately. Any Mana Burn that deals even 1 Aggravated leaves a permanent Mana Burn mark; what it looks like, how visible it is, and whether it changes anything are up to the Storyteller. Repeated burns can leave lasting deformities.

Mana Sickness

1 dot of Mana covers the world's baseline density comfortably. Some places are far denser: ancient magical sites, areas near Void openings, and places where something significant happened to the local mana. Elevated density deals Bashing damage; extreme concentration deals Lethal. The Storyteller decides what counts as elevated or extreme.

Direct summons from other worlds who arrive with no Mana take Bashing damage from the world itself until they develop 1 dot through play.

Mana and Voidlings

Voidlings feed on mana. Ambient mana sustains them at a basic level, but strong Mana signatures draw them. A party with several high-Mana characters in Void-adjacent territory should expect concentrated Voidling attention.

Mana After Death

When a character dies without choosing to, the mana they held counts down by 1 each day, and resurrection is possible until it runs out (Chapter 13).
