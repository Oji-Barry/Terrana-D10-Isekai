Introduction

Gear does not make the adventurer, but it helps. This chapter covers what things cost, what you can carry into a fight, the weapons and armor of modern Terrana, the everyday tools and magic that fill a delver's pack, and the tables a Storyteller rolls on when a party finds something worth keeping.

The weapons and prices here describe modern Terrana. Games set in the distant past, a fallen future, or the space era change what is available and what it costs (Chapter 16).

Money

Monsera's influence has standardized currency across the major trade routes. Coins are the everyday money of Terrana, and mana crystals of matching value change hands just as freely, since every implement in the world runs on them. The values in our world's dollars are only a guide for the table.

Item Ratings and Prices

Every weapon, piece of armor, tool, and magic item has a Rating from 1 to 7, matching the same F through S ranks used everywhere else. A Rating adds to the rolls the item is made for: a weapon's Rating adds to its damage, armor's Rating adds to your BDT, and a set of lockpicks adds its Rating to Sleight of Hand rolls to open a lock.

Weapons and Armor: A weapon or a piece of armor gets its Rating from its rank alone: F rank is +1, E is +2, D is +3, C is +4, B is +5, A is +6, and S is +7. A rusty F rank greatsword and an F rank dagger both add 1 to damage; what makes the greatsword different is its damage Attribute, its properties, and the Style it uses. Ordinary store-bought weapons and armor are usually F or E rank. A rank and S rank pieces are true masterworks, rarely seen and almost never for sale.

Other Items: Tools, rings, and other gear also have a rank, but their bonuses are usually smaller than a weapon's, as listed in their entries and on the loot tables.

Carrying Gear

Equipment Slots: Your sheet has a slot for each place you can wear or hold something: Headgear, Torso, Left Arm, Right Arm, Legs, Footwear, Main Weapon, Side Arm, Alt Weapons, and Stowed Items. Gloves, bracers, and rings go on the arm they are worn on; paired items go on both arms.

Weapons: You can carry at most 3 weapons and wield at most 2 at once. Swapping to an alt weapon costs 1 AP or your attack action that turn.

The Belt: Before combat, set up to 3 items on your belt for quick use. The first belt item you use in a turn is free, the second costs 1 AP, and the third costs 2 more AP, so using all three in one turn costs 3 AP. Items are used during the Move or Use Item phases; moving and using an item in the same phase costs AP.

Belt Slots 4 to 7: Extra belt slots open only when an item or ability grants them, such as Quickcut's Hidden Steel or Windreader's Quiver Belt, each of which opens slot 4 for one kind of item.

Stowed Items: Anything not worn, wielded, or on your belt is stowed. Retrieving a stowed item costs 1 AP or your attack action.

Weapons

How Weapons Work

Attack: Your attack roll is the Attribute + Ability + Combat Style listed for the weapon's Style (Chapter 12). Without the Style, roll the Attribute + Ability alone.

Damage: Your damage pool is the weapon's damage Attribute + its Rating, which comes from its rank (+1 at F up to +7 at S). Add the attack's excess successes and compare the total to the target's BDT, LDT, and ADT.

Damage Attribute: Each weapon lists the Attribute that drives its damage. Most use Strength or Dexterity, and firearms and bows use Perception, but some weapons use another Attribute entirely: a whip that works by trickery might use Manipulation, and a focus weapon might use Intelligence.

Range: Melee weapons hit adjacent zones, or one zone farther with the Reach property. Thrown weapons and objects can be thrown accurately up to double your Strength in zones, fewer for large or heavy things (Storyteller's call). Bows, guns, and ranged spells have no set range: they can hit anywhere on the battlefield you have line of sight to.

Line of Sight and Cover: Draw a straight line from the center of your square to the center of the target's square. If it passes through both the front and the back of the square the target is hiding behind, the target has full cover and cannot be hit. If it only crosses a side and the back of that square, or a corner, the target has half cover: -2 dice to the attack. Otherwise the shot is clear.

AP: Most weapons attack with your normal attack action. A weapon with an AP cost needs that much AP spent on top of the attack.

Properties

Concealable: Small enough to hide easily. +2 dice to keep it hidden when searched.

Light: Can be wielded in your off hand alongside another weapon.

Loud: Firing it gives away your position, even if you are hidden.

Mounted: Must be braced, set on a stand, or fired from a vehicle. You cannot move in a turn you fire it.

Reload (X): After X attacks, spend 1 AP or your attack action to reload.

Reach: Can hit a target one zone farther than adjacent.

Thrown: Can be thrown up to double your Strength in zones, using the Style's Primary pool.

Two-Handed: Needs both hands. You cannot hold a shield or a second weapon.

Versatile: Can be used one-handed or two-handed. With Silver Arc's Versatile Grip, two-handed adds half your dots to damage.

A mage's focus is a weapon for spells. Like a weapon's Rating, a focus adds +1 at F up to +7 at S to the damage of spells from the school it boosts, and the Rating comes from its rank. You still pay each spell's full mana cost. Each focus names its school, such as Tidecaller's Wand (C rank): +4 Water Magic. Rare ones boost two or more schools. A focus only boosts the casting roll itself when its entry says so, and some powerful ones hold a specific spell you can cast without mana or by spending a stored charge. Foci are artifacts, not implements.

Armor

Only armor worn on the body and the head adds to your Armor, and your Armor is added to your BDT (Fortitude + Armor). Arms, legs, rings, gloves, boots, and the like give other properties instead, such as bonus dice or bonus ability dots, and the most expensive pieces can even grant attribute dots.

Armor Rating: Like weapons, armor gets its Rating from its rank: F +1 up to S +7.

Armor Classes: The class decides which ranks a piece can come in. Light armor runs from F to D (Armor 1 to 3), Medium from D to B (3 to 5), and Heavy from B to S (5 to 7).

Who Can Wear What: Your Physical column limits what you can wear. Aversion: Light armor only. Standard: Light or Medium. Affinity: any armor. The same limit applies to shields.

Helmets: A helmet adds 1 Armor; a full helm adds 2, but only with Heavy body armor.

Magic and Armor: Armor can be enchanted for added effects, but only rarely to add more Armor. Points added by magic count as the magic part of your BDT, which Magic Penetration can ignore.

Potions and Mana Crystals

Healing comes only from combination magic, so a healing potion is a small implement: a combination healing spell bottled by a circle mage. Drinking one is a belt item. It gives you a PHP (Pool of Healing Points) at its healing tier that lasts until the end of your next turn (Chapter 13).

Mana crystals hold mana. Besides serving as money, a crystal can be used as a belt item to recover mana equal to its size, which uses it up. Recovery never takes you above your Mana Pool.

Vehicles and Mounts

Driving and Riding: Vehicles use the Pilot ability and living mounts use Ride. A vehicle or mount's Rating adds to those rolls, and its Speed is how many zones it moves per turn in combat.

Flight: Anything that flies moves at double its normal movement. Mechanical flight (airships, flying cars, and other machines) must use all of its movement every turn or begin to fall.

Vehicle Armor: People inside a closed vehicle add its Armor to their BDT against attacks from outside.

Magic Items

Magic items come in two kinds: implements and artifacts. The difference is whether someone designed the magic or the world put it there.

Implements

An implement is made on purpose to produce a specific effect, usually with circle magic. Implements are part of everyday life in Terrana: microwaves, fridges, lamps that never need oil, and storage pouches made with Spatial Magic are all implements. In games set in the future or in space, replicators and similar devices are implements too.

An implement does what it was designed to do. Its effect is known, repeatable, and the same every time. Using one costs mana each time, but it never needs attunement.

Artifacts

An artifact is an item that spent time somewhere with a high density of mana, such as a dungeon, and soaked up random magical abilities. Any item can become an artifact this way, from an ordinary object to an implement. No one designs an artifact's abilities, and two artifacts that started as the same item can end up nothing alike.

Attunement: To use an artifact you must attune to it, which holds part of your Max Mana Per Turn equal to its Rating for as long as you stay attuned. Once attuned, it needs no further mana to use.

Augmentations

An augmentation is a magical or mechanical enhancement built into the body or into a weapon with an augment slot, made with the Augmentation circle magic (Chapter 13). Body augmentations work like a natural limb while attuned and hold M/T equal to their Rating. Arcane Enhancements bought as Merits (Chapter 9) work differently and lower M/T permanently.

Spells Held in Items

A spell held in an implement or artifact, including a combination spell, does not require the user to meet its dot prerequisites. The item has the required dots built in; the user simply provides the mana.

Loot

Rolling for Loot

When a party finds treasure, the Storyteller can choose it or roll for it. Rolling takes three steps:

1. Loot Rank: Roll a d10 on the Loot Rank table for where the party is. This sets the item's rank, F through S.

2. What You Found: Roll a d100 to see what kind of item it is.

3. Bonus: Roll a d10 on the Bonus table for the item's rank. Where a bonus says "an ability" or "an attribute," roll on the Which Ability or Which Attribute table, or pick one that fits the item.

A weapon or armor gets its Rating from its rank (F +1 to S +7), and the bonus table adds something extra on top. Coin, potions, and crystals skip step 3. An item of B rank or higher, or any item found deep in a dungeon, may also roll on Artifact Quirks.
