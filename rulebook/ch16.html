Introduction

Every rule in this book works in any age. The rest of the book describes modern Terrana, about 150 years after the Slime King's arrival, but the same dice, the same Combat Styles, and the same magic can run a game in a dark age of swords and war, in the ruins of a fallen future, or among the stars.

This chapter describes three alternate eras. Each one lists what changes from the modern game: the technology, the gear and prices, which Backgrounds and guilds exist, and the factions that matter. Anything not mentioned works exactly as it does in the rest of the book.

Choosing an Era: The Storyteller picks the era before character creation, since it changes what players can buy and which Backgrounds make sense. Players should know the era before choosing a Trope or a Past Life Occupation.

The Distant Past

Long before Monsera, before the Slime King, before guilds ran governments and crystals ran kitchens, the world was at war. A game in the distant past is set during or just after the Great Hyuman-Demon War, when Hyumans and the peoples of the Demon Continent fought across the known world. It is a dark age: kingdoms rise and burn, roads are dangerous, magic is rare and feared, and a sword is worth more than a title.

What Changes

Technology: No crystal engines, no CCN, no guns, and almost no implements. Light comes from fire, travel is on foot, by horse, or by sail, and news moves as fast as a rider.

Combat Styles: Sundown and Iron Rain do not exist. Every other Style does, though under other names and taught by masters rather than schools.

Magic: The seven schools work as written, but formal study is rare. Most casters learn from a single master, and circle magic is closely guarded. Expect Storytellers to limit which combination spells anyone has discovered.

Backgrounds: Hacker and Subscribers do not exist. Guild Standing does not exist either; there are no cabinet guilds yet. Use Allies, Mentor, Nobility, Followers, and Street Cred instead.

Monsera and Its Institutions: Monsera, the Hero Rangers Corps, Monsera University, and the Grand Dungeon do not exist. The Grand Dungeon has not been built yet; the Earth God made it much later.

Gear and Prices: Use the weapons and armor in Chapter 14 except firearms, crystal-powered plate, and anything that runs on mana crystals. Coins vary from kingdom to kingdom, but the values in Chapter 14 still work as a guide. Healing potions are rare and cost at least twice as much.

Heritages: Every heritage exists, but Hyumans and demons are at war, so a character's heritage decides where they are welcome. Storytellers may rule some heritages unavailable or hidden.

Campaign Ideas

Behind the Lines: A small band is sent across the front to bring back a captured mage before the demon armies learn what she knows.

The Last Village: The war has moved on, but the village is starving and a monster has learned that no soldiers are coming.

Oathbreakers: Deserters from both sides form a company that sells its swords to whoever pays, until one job asks too much.

A Fallen Future

Something ended the modern world. The CCN went dark, the crystal engines stopped, and the cities emptied. Generations later, people live among the ruins of a civilization they barely understand, and today's ordinary technology has become a collection of ancient relics.

What caused the fall is left open. Each Storyteller decides, and the players may never learn the whole truth. Some possibilities:

A Great Breach: The Rift tore wide open and the Void poured through.

The Seeming Spills Over: The nightmare half of the Void World broke its balance and seeped into Terrana.

A New God War: Something awoke that the world was not ready to fight.

The Mana Fails: Ambient mana thinned, and everything built on it collapsed at once.

What Changes

Relics: Modern items (guns, CCN crystals, crystal engines, implements) are relics. A working relic counts as an artifact. A broken one needs a Crafts or Artifacts roll and parts to repair, and the Storyteller decides what still works.

Ammunition and Crystals: Bullets and charged mana crystals are scarce. Track ammunition carefully, and treat mana crystals as the most valuable currency there is.

Money: Coins are worth what someone will trade for them. Barter is common: food, water, ammunition, medicine, and crystals are the real currency. Resources above 3 is rare and usually means control of a settlement or a working relic.

Guilds and Factions: The cabinet guilds are gone. Replace Guild Standing with standing in a settlement or faction, using the same 1 to 7 scale.

The World: Ruined cities, Void-scarred wastes, mutated beasts, and fortified settlements. Old dungeons may be the safest places left, or the most dangerous.

Magic: Magic works as written and may be the only reliable power left. Whether mages are honored or hunted depends on what caused the fall.

Campaign Ideas

The Signal: An old CCN tower has started broadcasting again, and every faction in the wastes wants to reach it first.

Water Rights: A settlement holds the only working water purifier for a hundred miles, and someone is trying to steal it.

Into the Old City: The ruins of Monsera hold relics no one has touched in a century, and the things that live there now.

The Space Era

A researcher at Monsera University discovers that the energy signal of the Rift matches energy coming from far out in the sky. The Hero Rangers Corps forms a Sky Division to follow it, travel to other worlds, and connect their peoples. Games in the space era take place hundreds to a thousand years after that discovery.

By then many planets and species have been met. Most are friendly to the HRC; some are not. Space travel has become a hot commodity for anyone seeking adventure, and ships, while still pricey, are common. Terrana itself remains fully playable: Monsera is the HRC's home world and the heart of an interstellar CCN.

The heavens above Terrana, as charted by the HRC Sky Division. Every world in this chapter lies inside Terrana's bubble, between the sky and its outer wall.

Space Above Terrana

Air: Space above Terrana is not a vacuum. It is thin, cold, and windy, but it can be breathed, and a person can stand on an open deck among the stars without a helmet.

Gravity: Gravity always pulls toward Terrana, as though it were the center of everything, but the pull weakens the farther you go. Out among the worlds you are drawn to whatever large thing is closest: your ship, a station, or the nearest planet.

Speed and Wind: Most ships are airtight, because at high speed the wind would tear the air and anything loose right out of them. At a normal pace an open deck is safe.

Sailing Ships: Old-style wooden warships still sail between the worlds, driven by great Air Magic circles fixed to their masts and hulls. Space pirates are very real.

The Powers of the Sky

The Hero Rangers Corps Sky Division: The HRC's exploration fleet, much like a starfleet of explorers and diplomats. Its starships chart new worlds, keep the peace, and fight the Kaiju and voidlings that still come out of the dark. HRC ranks run 1 to 7 as on Terrana, and Guild Standing in the HRC is your rank.

The Mercenaries Guild: What the Adventurers Guild is on Terrana, the Mercenaries Guild is in space. Its members are freelance crews who pilot their own ships and take whatever work pays: escort runs, salvage, bounties, and jobs nobody asks questions about. Its great hall is on Kordrane.

The Free Reaches: Independent worlds and stations that answer to no crown. Kordrane, a rust-red mercenary port ringed by orbital stations, is its busiest world.

Vessaril: A green garden world and the HRC's first ally. Vessaril is peaceful, beautiful, and fiercely protective of its forests.

The Silver Court of Lunaria: A kingdom on Terrana's own Moon, hidden from the surface for most of history. Its court is elegant and ceremonial, its royal line is guarded by sworn protectors with their own names and colors, and its people draw on the light of the Moon itself. The Silver Court sees itself as the Moon's ancient guardian and treats the HRC as a younger but welcome ally.

The Aurelion Dominion: A great space monarchy ruled from the ringed throne world of Aurelion. Like the other astral kingdoms, it spans several planets, each held by a noble house sworn to the throne: desert worlds rich in rare crystals, ocean worlds, and fortress moons. Its houses scheme against each other constantly, and the Dominion's favor is worth more than gold.

The Varkesh Throne: A rival crown openly hostile to the HRC, ruling a dim cluster of worlds near the Sun's far side. The Varkesh see Terrana's expansion into the sky as an invasion, and their fleets test the HRC's borders often.

Nyxhollow and the Outer Dark: Nyxhollow is a dark world full of the voidlings that sometimes break into Terrana, and the farthest the HRC has ever reached. Its energy resembles the Rift's, but the signal leads further still, into the Outer Dark, where no ship has gone and come back.

Campaign Ideas

Bounty on Kordrane: A Mercenaries Guild crew takes a simple escort job and discovers their passenger is an heir the whole Aurelion Dominion is hunting.

First Contact: An HRC starship answers a distress call from a world no chart shows, and the people there have been waiting for them.

The Moon Princess: The Silver Court's heir vanishes during a state visit to Monsera, and the party is all that stands between the HRC and an interplanetary incident.

Ships

Ships use the vehicle rules from Chapter 14. A ship's Rating comes from its rank like any other item and adds to the rolls made to fly and fight with it. Each size class needs a minimum crew to fly at all; a ship short on crew can still move, but it cannot use the stations that have no one in them.

Crew Stations

Pilot: Flies the ship. Pilot rolls for maneuvers, chases, and dodging fire.

Gunner: Fires the ship's weapons, using Perception + Ranged + Iron Rain.

Engineer: Keeps the ship running and repairs damage. Crafts or Implements rolls.

Sensors: Scans, tracks, and spots threats. Awareness rolls.

Captain: Commands and coordinates. Once per turn the captain can give one crew member +2 dice with a Presence or Diplomacy roll.

Ship Damage: A ship takes hits exactly like a character. Its BDT is its Armor + its Size, so small arms rarely scratch a big ship. Its Hull Track has the same three columns as a character's health track: a hit that meets the ship's BDT marks a Bashing box (dents and scrapes), its LDT a Lethal box (hull breaches), and its ADT an Aggravated box (critical systems). A fighter's track matches a person's (7 Bashing, 5 Lethal, 3 Aggravated), and each size above 1 adds 2 boxes to every column. A ship that fills its Hull Track is disabled, not destroyed, unless the Storyteller says otherwise.

Ship Size Classes

Hull Track boxes are Bashing / Lethal / Aggravated. Max Capacity is everyone the ship can carry, crew and passengers.

Space Era Gear

Everything in Chapter 14 still exists, though most of it is old-fashioned. Energy weapons use the same Styles as the weapons they replace, and their Rating comes from their rank like any other weapon.

Energy Weapons Are Attuned: Blasters and energy blades are implements, but unlike other implements they only work for someone attuned to them. Attuning holds part of your Max Mana Per Turn equal to the weapon's Rating for as long as you stay attuned. An unattuned blaster or energy blade cannot be used at all.

Playable Species

Many species live among the stars. A player can design their own with the Storyteller, using the Spacefaring Species template, or choose one of the optional species below. Every species follows the same pattern as the heritages in Chapter 8: a lifespan, an Innate Attribute, an Innate Flaw, an Attribute Flaw, a few Heritage Merit options, and a Heritage magic.

Spacefaring Species (Template)

Lifespan: Choose one tier with the Storyteller: 100, 200, 400, or 1000 years.

Innate Attribute: Choose one attribute that fits the species with the Storyteller.

Innate Flaw: Choose one drawback with the Storyteller, about as strong as the flaws in Chapter 8.

Attribute Flaw: Choose one attribute group that can never be your Affinity column.

Heritage Merits: Pick broad options the way Demon does: a free specialty, a natural sense, a natural weapon, or a tolerance to one environment.

Heritage Magic: Design it with the Storyteller following the Tribal Magic guidelines in the Demon heritage (Chapter 8): seven ranks, two choices per rank, mana equal to rank.

Gravkin

Who They Are: Short, dense, immensely strong people from heavy-gravity worlds in the Free Reaches. Many crew mercenary ships as loaders and enforcers.

Lifespan: 200 years.

Innate Attribute: Strength.

Innate Flaw: Heavy Frame: cannot swim, and -1 die on Acrobatics rolls.

Attribute Flaw: Mental.

Heritage Merit (choose 1): Dense Frame: Grants a free specialty in Endurance called Dense Frame, providing a bonus die to resist being knocked back, knocked Prone, or crushed. Gravity-Born: Grants a free specialty in Athletics called Gravity-Born, providing a bonus die to lift, carry, and throw, and to move in strong or shifting gravity.

Heritage Magic: Weight of Worlds. Gravity: growing heavier, anchoring, pulling, and crushing.

Heritage Magic: Weight of Worlds

Gravkin grow up where the ground pulls hard, and they carry that pull with them. Weight of Worlds makes the caster heavier or lighter at will, drags enemies down, and turns a dropped fist into a falling star.

Primary: Strength + Athletics + Weight of Worlds

Secondary: Fortitude + Endurance + Weight of Worlds

Extra mana: Damage only. 1 die per mana, up to your Weight of Worlds dots or Max Mana Per Turn, whichever comes first.
